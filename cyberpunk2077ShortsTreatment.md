# 90s Skateboard Video Editing & Attention-Holding Skill

## Core Philosophy
1990s skateboarding videos (e.g., *Video Days*, *Plan B: Questionable*, *Mouse*, *The End*, *Welcome to Hell*) pioneered modern short-form attention mechanics decades before TikTok or YouTube. They rejected traditional Hollywood slow-burn storytelling in favor of high-density action, raw authenticity, rhythmic pacing, and visual juxtaposition. 

When applying this skill to a series of videos, treat the edit as a **visual mixtape** rather than a linear documentary.

---

## 1. Visual & Camera Directives

### Aspect Ratio & Framing
* **4:3 Pillarboxing:** Convert 16:9 modern footage into a strict `4:3` frame (`1440x1080` or `960x720`). Use hard black sidebars without blurred background fills.
* **Simulated Fisheye Vignette:** On wide-angle or high-energy action shots, apply subtle barrel distortion and a hard circular black vignette around the corners to mimic a Sony VX1000 paired with a Century Optics "Death Lens" (`0.3x` ultra-wide).
* **Low-Angle Follow Perspective:** Prioritize footage shot from knee-height or ground-level moving alongside the subject to amplify speed and scale.

### Color Grading & Analog Texture
* **VX1000 / MiniDV Color Profile:**
  * Crush blacks slightly and warm up the midtones/highlights.
  * Slightly boost saturation on primary colors (reds, blues, yellows) while keeping skin tones raw and unpolished.
  * Reduce dynamic range slightly so bright skies clip naturally rather than looking overly HDR-processed.
* **Analog Artifacts:**
  * Overlay subtle `480i` scanlines, light magnetic tape grain, and occasional chromatic aberration along high-contrast edges.
  * Insert brief VHS tracking glitches, tape static, or lens flares exclusively on hard scene transitions.

---

## 2. Pacing, Rhythm & Attention-Holding Mechanics

### Zero-Fluff Hook (The 0–3 Second Rule)
* **Cold Open:** Start immediately with a high-impact moment, a raw audio burst (e.g., wheels hitting concrete, a laugh, or a tape deck clicking play), or an absurd micro-skit. Never start with a slow fade-in or a generic logo animation.

### Micro-Arc Pacing (The "Part" Structure)
Structure each video in the series like a classic skate "part" (typically 2 to 4 minutes, or scaled down proportionally for 30–60 second shorts):
1. **The Opener (0–15%):** High-energy introduction that establishes the subject's personality and sets the tempo.
2. **The Montage / Flow State (15–65%):** Rapid-fire sequence of smaller wins, process shots, and rhythmic B-roll cut strictly to the beat of the soundtrack.
3. **The B-Roll / Skit Break (65–75%):** A sudden drop in music to showcase behind-the-scenes banter, failures ("slams"), or lo-fi humor. This resets viewer attention before the climax.
4. **The Ender / Hammer (75–100%):** Escalating stakes culminating in the single hardest, cleanest, or most impressive moment of the video.

### Cut Timing & Cadence
* **Action-Driven Cuts:** Cut on movement. Leave `0.5` to `1.0` second of lead-in before the key action and `1.0` to `1.5` seconds of aftermath (the "roll-away" or immediate reaction) so the viewer feels the completion of the effort.
* **Rhythmic Escalation:** Shorten average shot length (`ASL`) from `3–4 seconds` during setup phases down to `1–2 seconds` during high-energy sequences.
* **The "Slam" Principle (Vulnerability = Retention):** Never edit out all mistakes. Show 2–3 failed attempts or frustrating moments right before the final success to build genuine tension and payoff.

---

## 3. Audio Design & Music Sync

### Soundtrack Selection & Mixing
* **Genre Palette:** Underground boom-bap hip-hop, 90s indie/lo-fi rock, punk, funk, or jazz breaks.
* **Diegetic Audio Priority:** Do not mute the original camera microphone. Keep raw ambient audio (footsteps, wind, board/tool impacts, background chatter) sitting at roughly `-12dB` to `-6dB` underneath the music track so the viewer feels physically present.
* **Music Dropouts:** Hard-cut the music right before a major climax or comedic moment, letting the raw room/street audio carry the tension, then slam the beat back in on impact.

---

## 4. Typography & Graphic Overlays

* **Title Cards:** Use simple, utilitarian fonts (e.g., *Helvetica Bold*, *Courier*, *Impact*, or *VCR OSD Mono*) in stark white or yellow text over black frames or raw B-roll.
* **Lower Thirds:** Introduce subjects or locations with minimal, unpolished text in the bottom-left corner—no bouncy motion-graphics templates.
* **Super 8 / Hi8 Cutaways:** Sprinkle 1–2 second flashes of gritty, overexposed film texture or timestamped camcorder overlays (`PLAY ►`, `SP`, `NOV 18 1996`) as visual punctuation between chapters.

---

## 5. Step-by-Step Execution Workflow for AI / Editors

1. **Ingest & Crop:** Reframe all source clips to `4:3`. Stabilize overly shaky handheld footage only slightly—keep natural handheld micro-shake intact.
2. **Select the Beat:** Lock in a driving audio track first. Mark downbeats and snare hits on the timeline.
3. **Sequence the "Ender" First:** Identify the strongest clip in the footage and place it at the end. Build the preceding sequence to escalate toward it.
4. **Inject B-Roll & Fails:** Insert 2–3 candid moments, bloopers, or setup shots mid-way through to break visual monotony.
5. **Apply Analog Pass:** Add the MiniDV color LUT, subtle film grain, fisheye vignette on wide shots, and clean mono/stereo diegetic audio dips.
6. **Review for Momentum:** Watch the cut with the sound off—if any single shot drags for more than 4 seconds without new visual information or movement, trim it.

# Notes: how this treatment applies to the Cyberpunk 2077 playthrough

Everything above this line is the treatment as it was uploaded, unchanged. Everything below is notes. The decisions were made on November 18, 2026. Where a decision changes part of the treatment, the treatment above is left as it is and the change is written here.

## Decided

- **Shorts only.** The treatment applies to YouTube Shorts. The full mission videos stay in the straight style.
- **One part-length Short per mission,** built in the treatment's four sections: the opener, the montage, the skit break and the ender. Each mission keeps its full video as well.
- **Up to 3 minutes, not 4.** YouTube treats a video as a Short only if it runs 3 minutes or less, so a part runs 2 to 3 minutes.
- **Deaths go in both places.** A few deaths before the success stay in the full video. They also go into that mission's part Short, in the skit break. There are no separate death Shorts. This replaces the earlier rule that every game over, surprise and dumb death is cut out of the full videos into Shorts.
- **Vertical crop, no 4:3.** Shorts are cropped straight to 9:16. The treatment's 4:3 frame with black sidebars is dropped. (YouTube also requires a Short to be vertical or square.)
- **The look:** the MiniDV color grade, the analog texture (scanlines and tape grain; VHS glitches and static on hard transitions only), and the fisheye vignette on wide, high-energy shots.
- **Not chosen:** the title cards and lower thirds.
- **The camcorder overlay's date** uses the zine's YYYY:DDD form (year, then the day of the year in three digits), and shows the day the Short is published.
- **No narration** in Shorts. Music, game sound and the overlays only.
- **Music:** the game's own soundtrack.
- **Camera:** "Videos are confined to the way they were shot in gameplay. We can't alter the physics of that." So the treatment's camera directives (the low-angle follow shot, knee and ground height) don't apply. Only effects added in the edit do.

## Where the treatment meets the footage

These are notes, not decisions.

1. **The game's music is already in the footage.** The game's audio is recorded as one mixed track, so whatever score or radio was playing is under any track added in the edit. A music dropout before the ender drops only the added track.
2. **Music rights.** CD Projekt allows its own music in videos about its games, and says some music in the game isn't theirs. Check each track before it's used. Whether "Disable Copyrighted Music" was on for the hours already recorded isn't known.
3. **The vertical crop keeps about a third of the width.** A 9:16 crop from a 16:9 frame keeps about 32% of it. The HUD at the edges goes, and so may dialogue choices and anything off-center. Check each shot.
4. **The clean-plate settings fit.** The recording checklist turns the game's own film grain and chromatic aberration off, so the analog texture is added in the edit, where it can be kept to Shorts.
5. **The fisheye vignette is an edit effect.** The field of view is whatever the game was set to when the footage was recorded.
6. **DaVinci Resolve.** The checklist's Shorts step uses Smart Reframe, which may need the paid Studio version. Check which of the grade, grain and distortion effects the free version has.
7. **Deaths in the full videos** sit inside the crew's story, where V can't die. How a full video's narration treats a death the story says didn't happen isn't decided.
8. **What counts as a mission's ender** isn't set.
9. **Whether the crew's on-screen text lines appear in Shorts** isn't set.
10. **YouTube's monetization.** Shorts with no narration rely on the edit for the original work YouTube's reused content policy looks for.
