# Fact check: Sketch What You See (handbook and workbook)

Checked 26 November 2026.

**How:**
- Quotations from the 1903 manual and from Fulmer (1917) were checked word for word against the scanned text.
- Ruskin was checked against the 1857 text.
- Other claims were checked against museum, library and reference sources (listed below).

## Corrected

| Claim as written | Finding | Now reads |
|---|---|---|
| Eye-tracking studies show artists glance more often "than beginners, who look once and draw from memory" | Overstated. The supporting study is Cohen (2005), "Look little, look often": gaze frequency predicted accuracy | Cites Cohen 2005 and states its finding; added to sources |
| Nicolaides' book "has never gone out of print" | Unverifiable | "is still in print" |
| Bargue's *Cours de dessin* published "between 1866 and 1871" | Sources disagree (1866–71, 1868–71, 1866–76) | "published in parts by Goupil from the late 1860s"; about 200 plates |
| Egyptian grids "more than four thousand years ago" | The earliest surviving grids are Middle Kingdom, Dynasty 11 | "about four thousand years ago — the earliest surviving grids date from the Middle Kingdom" |
| Brunelleschi's demonstration "probably around 1415–20" | Sources give 1413–1420 | "some time between about 1413 and 1420" |
| Sandby at Woolwich "from 1768 to 1796" | End date given as 1796 or 1799 | "from 1768 until the late 1790s" |
| *Kim*: Lurgan "shows him a tray of jewels … trains him until he can describe every stone" | In the novel a younger boy beats Kim at the Jewel Game, and Kim practises for days | Rewritten to match the novel |
| Lecoq de Boisbaudran: *The Training of the Memory in Art* (1847, expanded 1862) | The original is *L'Éducation de la mémoire pittoresque* (1848; 2nd ed. 1862); the English translation is from 1911. Rodin was also a pupil | Corrected title, dates and pupils (text and sources) |
| Southern Cross: pole "roughly four and a half times" the Cross's length | Guides say 4 (EarthSky) or 4½ | "about four times … (some guides say four and a half)" |
| Compass "in use at sea in Europe by the thirteenth century" | True but vague; the first European description is Neckam, c. 1187 | "first described in the 1180s, by … Alexander Neckam"; Shen Kuo's *Dream Pool Essays* (1088) named |
| Snellius "in 1615" | The survey was made in 1615 and published in 1617 | "(published in 1617)" added |
| Barker's "panoramas of Edinburgh and London drew crowds in the 1790s" | The Edinburgh showing (1788) had little success; the London view succeeded; the Leicester Square rotunda opened in 1793 | Rewritten with those facts |
| Workbook S6.8: things look closer "across water or a valley" | A fully visible valley makes distance look *greater*; only a hidden dip makes it look less | "across water or a dip you cannot see into" |

## Confirmed

- **Quotations:**
  - Ruskin, *The Elements of Drawing* (1857): the "innocence of the eye" passage, word for word
  - Cézanne to Bernard, 15 April 1904
  - every quotation from the 1903 manual and Fulmer
- **Perception and drawing:**
  - Gombrich, *Art and Illusion* (1960), "making comes before matching"
  - Rubin's figure–ground thesis (Copenhagen, 1915)
  - Nicolaides (1891–1938) taught about 15 years at the Art Students League; *The Natural Way to Draw*, 1941, published after his death
  - Hogarth (1753), Fitts (1954), Helmholtz, Gibson (1950)
- **Perspective:**
  - Masaccio's *Holy Trinity*, c. 1427
  - Alberti (1435)
  - Dürer's treatise: 1525 first edition, 1538 expanded edition with the draughtsman woodcuts
  - Vredeman de Vries' *Perspective*, 1604–05
  - Brook Taylor (1715) is credited with first using "vanishing point" (one scholarly source; phrased as "usually credited")
- **Form and composition:**
  - Schön's block figures (1538) and Cambiaso's block figures
  - Rule of thirds first written down by J. T. Smith (1797)
- **Value and colour:**
  - Chevreul (1839, Gobelins dye works)
  - Munsell (1905, value 0–10)
  - Dow's *Composition* (1899, notan)
  - Payne's *Composition of Outdoor Painting* (1941)
  - Albers (1963)
- **Sketching and reportage:**
  - Constable's cloud studies (1821–22), annotated with date, time and wind
  - Grinnell's field-note method (notebook, journal, species accounts, catalogue)
  - Baudelaire's essay on Constantin Guys (*Le Figaro*, 1863)
  - Urban Sketchers (Flickr group November 2007, Gabriel Campanario; now 500+ chapters)
  - Cézanne's Mont Sainte-Victoire: "more than sixty" works (National Galleries of Scotland)
- **Military and survey drawing:**
  - Weir taught drawing at West Point from 1834 to 1876
  - Sandby: Military Survey of Scotland from 1747
- **Memory and scouting:** Kim's Game in *Scouting for Boys* (1908)
- **Landscape painting:** Guo Xi's three distances (high, deep, level)
- **Direction and navigation:**
  - Shen Kuo (1088)
  - Polaris less than 1° from the pole (about 0.7°)
  - Naismith's rule (1892)
- **Surveying:**
  - Gemma Frisius' triangulation (1533, appended to Apian's *Cosmographia*)
  - plane table described by 1551 (Foullon); Praetorius c. 1590
  - Ordnance Survey triangulation 1783/1791–1853
- **Panoramas:** Barker's panorama patent (June 1787)

## Not independently checked

These are widely documented or general:
- Vasari's Giotto story (1550)
- Leonardo on aerial perspective
- Caravaggio
- the *Illustrated London News* (1842)
- Winslow Homer for *Harper's Weekly*
- squared drawings by Raphael
- the speed of sound, pace length and walking speed
