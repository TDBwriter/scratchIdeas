SEVEN

The lamp room was colder than she remembered, and the glass hummed faintly in the wind as if it were trying to tell her something it had told a hundred keepers before. She set the kettle on the little stove and waited, counting the seconds between each sweep of the beam across the water.

Somewhere below, the radio crackled. It was only the weather, the same patient voice reading the same patient numbers, but tonight it sounded closer, like someone standing just outside the door. She did not get up. She told herself there was no one on the island but her and the gulls, and the gulls did not knock.

By the time the kettle sang, the fog had come in thick enough to swallow the beam a hundred yards out. She poured the tea, wrapped both hands around the cup, and opened the logbook to the last page in her father's handwriting.

Three words, underlined twice. She had read them every night for a month and still did not know what they meant.
