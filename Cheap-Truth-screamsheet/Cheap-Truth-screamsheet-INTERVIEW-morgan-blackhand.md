# Interview prep: Morgan Blackhand

**Status by 2077:** missing since 2023. Believed dead; not confirmed.
**Fits the department:** yes, as the "Heard you were dead" echo of the original's #17.

## What canon says
- The top Solo of Cyberpunk 2020: the core rulebook. *Solo of Fortune 2* ranks him first and credits him with *The Enforcer's Handbook*. Mike Pondsmith created him.
- Lost his right arm as a soldier in the Central American wars. Became a mercenary, mostly for Militech.
- In 2019 he stopped an attempt to kidnap Kerry Eurodyne and took all five kidnappers alive.
- In the Fourth Corporate War he led Militech special operations in the ocean war. He tried, and failed, to stop an Arasaka team from killing Rache Bartmoss.
- In August 2023 he joined the Militech strike on Arasaka Tower. On the roof, he stayed behind to fight Adam Smasher so his team could get out. The tower was then destroyed. His fate is unknown.
- Cyberpunk RED repeats rumors: that he reported to the US president, that he worked as a secret government asset, that he was seen in the cities that recovered first.
- In 2077 he has no drink at the Afterlife, because nobody is sure he's dead. A news story about a Japantown massacre quotes a survivor describing a portly man with a black cyberarm. A fan wiki says Jackie names him as a top solo.

Sources: Lore Fortress, "Morgan Blackhand"; Cyberpunk Wiki (via search results).

**Invented for the joke:** the price of water; "I started that one"; the invoicing joke; settling his own tab.

## Draft interview

## INTERVIEW WITH THE PROFESSIONAL

We got hold of Morgan Blackhand. Never mind how. There are things on the bottom shelf of the Afterlife that are best left unspoken. At any rate we had the gentleman at a back table in 2077 -- some 54 years after anybody last saw him. This took doing. The Afterlife names a drink after every dead legend, and there is no Morgan Blackhand on the menu, because nobody is sure he's dead. We ordered a glass of tap water and hoped.

Blackhand wore a plain gray jacket and the kind of boots that have walked out of more places than they walked into. One black cyberarm lay on the table like a tool he hadn't finished using. He looked rested, which worried us -- we had called him up from August 2023, about ten minutes before the roof.

CT: Mr. Blackhand -- may we call you Morgan? -- this is a great pleasure. We heard you were dead.

MB: (checking the exits) You hear a lot of things. You hear the one where I report to the President?

CT: Sure.

MB: Good. I started that one. It pays better than being dead.

CT: Preem. Can we get you anything? There's a Johnny Silverhand, a --

MB: Water. I'll drink in here when there's a drink with my name on it. Not before. (inspects the glass) They charge for this?

CT: Four eddies.

MB: For water. In my day a corp would at least shoot at you first.

CT: Let's talk about the work. You were the top solo of your time. The rankings say so.

MB: The rankings are written by people who sell rankings. I'll tell you what I was. I was on time. You know how rare that is in this business? I lost an arm for a flag in Central America. The flag never once asked how I was doing. Militech asked every month. That's the whole secret of corporate loyalty, kid. Direct deposit.

CT: 2019. Somebody tries to kidnap Kerry Eurodyne. You take all five of them alive. That's -- that's beautiful. That's mercy.

MB: That's invoicing. Dead men can't tell you who hired them, and then who do you bill for the follow-up? (warming up) Five live ones is five leads. Five leads is a quarter's work. I had a system.

CT: (uncomfortably) OK, that's nova. What about Rache Bartmoss? They say you tried to save him.

MB: I was late.

CT: You were never late.

MB: Once. (He looks at the black hand for a while.) It's the one that didn't pay.

CT: And the roof? Arasaka Tower? You stayed behind with Adam Smasher so your team could get out --

MB: Somebody holds the door. Usually the guy standing nearest. It isn't philosophy.

CT: There's a story out of Japantown. A massacre. One survivor. Says the killer was a portly man with a black cyberarm.

MB: Lot of black arms in this city.

CT: And portly?

MB: (stiffly) Lot of noodles.

CT: Mr. Blackhand, may we say something? This zine is for people who'd rather walk out of a room than shoot their way out. You took five men alive. You held the door. You're more than a solo -- you're an example. A professional with a code. You're one of us.

MB: You carry?

CT: No.

MB: You bill?

CT: It's free. It's not even copyrighted.

MB: (standing) Then you're not professionals. You're a hobby. (He counts four eddies onto the table, exactly.) And kid -- the day there's a drink in here with my name on it, don't order it. Somebody'll be lying.

(Blackhand leaves by a door the Afterlife does not have. He is the only guest we have ever had who settled his own tab.)
