# Cheap Truth: the serial

A numbered, multi-part story for the screamsheet, built from Watson's NCPD scanner jobs. It belongs to the zine alone: it reports only what an eyewitness in Night City could see.

Working title: **THE DEAD LEAVE DIRECTIONS**

---

## The larger story

In Watson the police post crimes on a scanner, and whoever shows up closes them. At six of those scenes somebody left a note about something hidden: a shard on a body, messages on a computer, details on a corpse. Each note leads to a stash somewhere else.

Each part follows one note from the scene to the stash. The last stash is named "Cops' Cash."

**What the whole serial shows:** the cycle of money and violence that corporate rule keeps turning. The scanner pays for violence. The violence protects somebody's payments. The money comes back round to the people who posted the job. Nobody on the street set it up, and everybody on the street keeps it going.

## Numbered, and still standing alone

Each part carries its number and the serial's title: "THE DEAD LEAVE DIRECTIONS, part 3 of 6." That is the only link between parts.

A part has to read complete to someone who has seen no other part:
- It names its own scene, people and place from scratch.
- It answers the three questions itself.
- It never says "last time," "as we showed" or "next."
- Part 6 states the cycle in full. It can't lean on parts 1 to 5 to have set it up.

---

## Headlines and tone: the samizdat sheet

The headlines above follow the pattern of a street-level underground sheet: a blunt phrase, a colon, then exactly who, where and what. They are local and they name the human cost.

Three habits of that kind of sheet, for the serial's text:

1. **Specifics, always.** Names, places, dates and amounts in place of "the police are corrupt." Every figure has to come from the game: the note's own words, the name on the stash, what the scanner pays.
2. **No "both sides."** The piece doesn't ask the NCPD or a corp for a comment. It assumes the arrangement is what it looks like.
3. **It ends with something to do.** Where a paper would run an ad, each part closes with one line of use to the reader: where to stand to see it for yourself, or how to carry a camera past a cop.

**A tension to settle:** the zine's voice is "style over substance," and this habit is dry and exact. One way through is a loud headline over a plain, specific body. The other is to keep the zine's voice throughout and add the specifics to it.

## Underneath: the real parallels

Unnamed, as the rules require. Each part can sit on one of these, and a reader who knows the real world will see it.

| The pattern | In Night City | Where it shows |
|---|---|---|
| **Private money turns public police into private guards.** Who gets a fast response, and who paid for it? | The department's cash sits on a gang's roof. | Part 6 |
| **Enforcement run for revenue:** bounties, quotas, fines. | The scanner pays strangers by the job. | Every part: who pays when a call is closed? |
| **Surveillance supplied by private hands.** Who is listening, and who sold them the ears? | Someone is reading the same scanner. | The optional seventh part |
| **Immunity.** An officer who serves the right people answers to nobody. | A stash with the police's name on it, and nobody asks. | Part 6 |

---

## How it sits in an issue

- **As a department.** Each part is one of an issue's optional departments, about 150 to 250 words with its photo links.
- **Where the stash is can be a Top Ten X.** The lines cross at the hiding place.
- **All six scenes are in Watson,** so the whole serial can be shot before The Heist.
- **Each part needs its NCPD job assigned to that issue,** and the rule for assigning side content is still undecided.
