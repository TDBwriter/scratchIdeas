# Interview prep: Mike Pondsmith, on censorship

**Status:** a living, real person: the creator of Cyberpunk.
**What this is:** a fourth-wall easter egg. It breaks test F1 and section 7 on purpose.
**The caricature:** a rich Streetkid who forgot his poor roots. Humorously cynical.

## What's on the record
- Created Cyberpunk, a tabletop RPG, in 1988. Founder of R. Talsorian Games. A consultant on *Cyberpunk 2077*.
- **He is in the game.** He voices Maximum Mike, the DJ of 107.3 Morro Rock Radio, whose show covers Night City's mysteries and the corporations' misdeeds. "Maximum Mike" is also his persona in the tabletop books. (Cyberpunk Wiki and cameo lists; fan sources.)
- Has said in several interviews that Cyberpunk is "a warning, not an aspiration," and that it's "inherently political."
- Cyberpunk 2020's tenets: style over substance, attitude is everything, always take it to the edge, break the rules.
- Nothing of his on censorship itself turned up. Everything he says here is invented.

## Keeping it fair
- **It caricatures a public role, not a private life.** The joke is the man who wrote the warning doing well out of it. "Streetkid" is the game's lifepath, and the piece says so: "It's on my sheet." It makes no claim about his real upbringing or money.
- **The class jokes stay class jokes.** No dialect, nothing that leans on who he is.
- **It says it's invented,** in the zine's own voice, in the last line.

Sources: Cyberpunk Wiki, "Mike Pondsmith"; Gamers Decide, "All Cameos in Cyberpunk 2077"; WatchMojo, "Top 10 Cameos"; NME, "a warning, not an aspiration"; PC Gamer, "everything is political"; VGC interview.

**Invented for the joke:** all of it, apart from the radio show, the four tenets and "a warning."

## Draft interview

## INTERVIEW WITH THE CREATOR

Saddened to learn that the man who wrote Night City must, like every founder this town has ever had, be long dead, we tried the unspeakable necromancy of the Afterlife's bottom shelf and asked for a post-mortem interview. Never mind how.

To our surprise we found Maximum Mike, of 107.3 Morro Rock Radio, apparently very much alive and climbing out of a private AV on the roof. He wore a street kid's jacket with the collar up. It had been tailored. The rips were hand-finished. His boots had never met a puddle. He looked wonderful -- nobody had called him up from anywhere. He was between meetings.

CT: Mr. Pondsmith -- may we call you Maximum? -- this is a great pleasure. We heard you were dead.

MM: (checking a very thin phone) Not a scratch. Who told you dead?

CT: Everybody who ever founded anything in this town is dead.

MM: That's because they stayed. (to his driver) Keep it running. (to us) What's good here? What are the kids drinking?

CT: A Johnny Silverhand's eighteen eddies.

MM: Eighteen! (whistles) Is that a lot? I honestly can't tell anymore. Get two. Put it on a -- do they still do tabs? Put it on a tab.

CT: You came up down here. A Streetkid.

MM: Hundred percent. Still am. It's on my sheet.

CT: Which block?

MM: The -- you know. With the wall. There was a guy with a cart. (snaps fingers) Great guy. I'm told they've put a boutique there now. I'm an investor.

CT: We wanted to ask you about censorship.

MM: Love it. Big topic. Huge. I want to be very clear: nobody censors me.

CT: Nova.

MM: I have people for that.

CT: Sorry?

MM: Standards. Legal. Brand. A whole floor. Nobody tells me what I can't say! They tell me before I say it. It's much more efficient. I haven't been censored in thirty years. I've been aligned.

CT: But your show. You go after the corps every night.

MM: Every night! Fearless. (to the driver) What's our read tonight? (to us) "This hour of corporate misdeeds is brought to you by" -- and then whoever. It rotates.

CT: The corps sponsor the show about what the corps did?

MM: Who else has the budget? Kid, that's not censorship. That's synergy. Censorship is when they stop you. I've never been stopped. I've been renewed.

CT: You wrote four rules once. Style over substance. Attitude is everything. Always take it to the edge. Break the rules.

MM: (fondly) Break the rules. God, I was hungry.

CT: Do you still?

MM: Break them? Constantly. Within guidelines. There's a PDF.

CT: You used to call all this a warning.

MM: It is a warning! Passionately. And I'll tell you something: warnings test through the roof. People cannot get enough of being warned. We're doing a second warning. Bigger. There's a collector's edition of the warning.

CT: Mr. Pondsmith, may we say something? You made this city. Every word we print, you said first. You're the father of this zine. You're one of us.

MM: (genuinely moved) That's beautiful. That's so street. Listen -- who has the license?

CT: The what?

MM: On the zine. Who do I talk to?

CT: Nobody. It's not copyrighted.

MM: (a long pause; to the driver) He's serious. (to us, gently, as to a child) Choomba. Choombas? Is it still choomba? You can't just say things for free. Somebody could repeat them.

CT: That's the idea.

MM: (backing toward the stairs) I remember being like you. I think. It's on my sheet somewhere.

(Maximum Mike leaves by AV, which is double-parked on the roof. We are informed that this interview has not been cleared, and that none of it happened. He made us up. It seemed only fair.)
