# Interview prep: Spider Murphy

**Status by 2077:** not found. One fan discussion says she survives the 2023 raid; nothing confirms her status in RED or 2077.
**Fits the department:** only if "believed dead" is stretched to "fate unknown." See the open questions in `Cheap-Truth-screamsheet-INTERVIEWS.md`.

## What canon says
- A Cyberpunk 2020 netrunner.
- She met Rache Bartmoss in her teens (he was 17) and became his apprentice, partner in crime and best friend.
- His style was flamboyant and aggressive. **Hers favored utility and non-lethal attacks.**
- She was part of the 2023 attack on Arasaka Tower. Her focus was destroying Arasaka's upgraded Soulkiller and setting Alt Cunningham free.
- She told Alt about Bartmoss's death. Alt gave her a piece of Soulkiller to use if needed.
- In *Firestorm: Shockwave* (an alternate timeline, per the wiki), she recovered Alt's personality file and threw it somewhere on the net to be retrieved later.
- She edited Bartmoss's writings after his death.
- In *CyberGeneration*, Bartmoss recruits players to find and rescue her.

Sources: Cyberpunk Wiki, "Spider Murphy" and "Altiera Cunningham" (via search results); Screen Rant, "Who Rache Bartmoss Really Was"; World Anvil (fan source, low weight).

**Invented for the joke:** the jukebox; her complaint about royalties and "a credit in small type"; "a deck that bit".

## Draft interview

## INTERVIEW WITH THE SAINT

We got hold of Spider Murphy. Never mind how. There are things on the bottom shelf of the Afterlife that are best left unspoken. At any rate we had the lady on the Afterlife's jukebox in 2077. Nobody could tell us whether she was dead, and the Afterlife has no drink for her, so we fed the jukebox a coin and left a port open.

Murphy came up on the screen in a worn flight jacket, with interface plugs at her temple. She looked the room over the way a runner looks at a building: where the wires go, and who's paying for them.

CT: Ms. Murphy -- Rache Bartmoss's famous apprentice! This is a great --

SM: Partner.

CT: Sorry?

SM: Apprentice is what they call it when they want the man to get the royalties. Go on.

CT: Preem. Can we get you anything?

SM: I'm a jukebox. (pause) Put on something without guitars.

CT: How did you two meet?

SM: I was a kid. He was a kid with money. CCI was paying him like a god to write Daemons, and he spent it like a seventeen-year-old, which he was. He taught me how to get into anything. I taught him you could leave without setting it on fire. One of those lessons took.

CT: That's what we wanted to ask about. Your style. Utility. Non-lethal. You never fried anybody --

SM: Frying people is loud. Loud brings NetWatch. Loud brings their friends next week. You leave a sysop alive and confused, he spends tomorrow explaining it to his boss and nobody believes him. That's free cover. Bodies are overhead.

CT: So it's -- economics.

SM: Everything's economics, honey. Rache did it for the art, and look where the art is. In a freezer.

CT: 2023. Arasaka Tower. You went in for Alt Cunningham.

SM: We went in to break Soulkiller before Arasaka shipped the upgrade. Alt came with it. (quietly) I had to tell her Rache was dead. Better me than a log file.

CT: And she gave you something.

SM: A piece of the thing that killed her. "Use it if you need to." Worst present I ever got, and Rache once gave me a deck that bit.

CT: After he died, you edited his writing.

SM: Somebody had to cut the parts where he insults the reader's mother. It was most of the parts. (brightening) You know what I got for editing the greatest netrunner who ever lived? A credit. In small type.

CT: Ms. Murphy, may we say -- we've always admired you? You went in, got the truth, got out, and left nobody dead on the floor. That's everything this zine believes in. You're practically our patron saint.

SM: Do saints get paid?

CT: Uh. No. Generally they get martyred.

SM: Then put me down as a contractor. (The jukebox flickers.) Kid, I wasn't kind. I was tidy. Don't make a religion out of a skill set.

CT: One last thing. Are you dead?

SM: Dead's a status flag. I set my own. Are you buying?

(The jukebox plays two seconds of a song nobody picked and returns to the menu. It kept our coin.)
