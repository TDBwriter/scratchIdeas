# Interview prep: Trace Santiago

**Status by 2077:** whereabouts unknown. Per the Cyberpunk Wiki, his portrait hangs in the Afterlife.
**Canon:** Cyberpunk RED, not Cyberpunk 2020. His father, Nomad Santiago, is the 2020 character.
**Fits the department:** only if it opens to RED characters and to "fate unknown."

## What canon says
- A Media in Cyberpunk RED, and one of the edgerunners in Mike Pondsmith's RED fiction (R. Talsorian Games, June 2020).
- The son of Nomad Santiago, who led the Aldecaldos nomad family in Cyberpunk 2020.
- The character was created with a real collaborator, merged with the existing Santiago family. **That collaborator is a real person and stays out of the interview.**
- A non-canon Cyberpunk V3 made him Nomad Santiago's grandson instead.
- In 2077: his whereabouts are unknown; his portrait is in the Afterlife.
- The RED Media role: a Media's power is Credibility, being believed.

Sources: R. Talsorian Games, "Cyberpunk Red: Meet the new Edgerunners" (June 12, 2020); Cyberpunk Wiki, "Trace Santiago," "Nomad Santiago," "Aldecaldos Members" (via search results).

## Why he matters to the zine
He's a Media, like V-media, and he works under his own name. *Cheap Truth* is anonymous. Trace is the one legend who can argue with Veritas about the craft itself.

**Invented for the joke:** that he takes over the interview; "I had a number"; his opinion of his portrait.

## Draft interview

## INTERVIEW WITH THE LEGEND

We got hold of Trace Santiago. Never mind how. There are things on the bottom shelf of the Afterlife that are best left unspoken. At any rate we had the gentleman at the bar in 2077. His portrait hangs on the Afterlife wall with the legends, and nobody could tell us whether that means he's dead. We ordered two of whatever the bar pours for people it isn't sure about.

Santiago had road dust on his boots and a recorder clipped where other people wear a gun. He looked like a man who has slept in a truck and liked it -- we had called him up from the Time of the Red, about 2045, when the city was still digging itself out. He looked at our notebook before he looked at us.

CT: Mr. Santiago, this is a great pleasure --

TS: Who are you with?

CT: CHEAP TRUTH.

TS: Circulation?

CT: Uh. It gets passed around.

TS: (writing it down) "Passed around." And you are?

CT: Veritas.

TS: That's not a name. That's a slogan.

CT: That's the point.

TS: (still writing) "Declined to give name."

CT: Excuse us -- we're interviewing you.

TS: Are you? Fine. Go.

CT: Tell us about your name.

TS: Santiago. My father's before it was mine. Nomad Santiago, Aldecaldos. I grew up in a convoy. You learn two things in a convoy: everybody's job matters, and nobody tells the truth about a road they haven't driven.

CT: And you became a Media.

TS: Somebody had to tell the cities what the roads looked like. The corps had the screens. The family had the trucks. I had a recorder and a face people could find. You know what a Media's got, kid? One thing. Credibility. People believe me because they know who I am, and where I'll be standing tomorrow when they come to complain.

CT: Who paid you?

TS: Everybody tried. That's how you know it's working. The trick isn't turning the money down. The trick is being known as the guy it doesn't work on, and then never checking. (pause) I had a number. Everybody's got a number. Mine never got offered. That's not virtue. That's luck.

CT: Here's our case for no name. We sign it, we're a target. We name a source, they're a body. The anonymity is the shield.

TS: I've made that speech. Bad nights, mostly. It works right up until somebody prints a lie and signs it "Veritas." Then which one's you?

CT: You can tell by the work.

TS: (laughs for some time) Oh, kid.

CT: You're on the wall in here. With the legends. Doesn't that make you one of ours?

TS: It makes me a picture in a bar. They never even asked. (He studies his own portrait.) That's not my good side. You want to be one of mine? Sign one story. One. See how it feels when they know where you live.

CT: We'll think about it.

TS: (closing the notebook) "Said they'd think about it." Thanks for your time.

(Santiago leaves with our interview. We have no idea where it ran.)
