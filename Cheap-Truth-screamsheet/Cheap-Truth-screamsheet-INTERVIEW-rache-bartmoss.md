# Interview prep: Rache Bartmoss

**Status by 2077:** dead. Sources disagree on how: an Arasaka team killed him, or his heart stopped in 2020 and a cryo freezer preserved him.
**Fits the department:** yes.

## What canon says
- Born in 1992. Netrunning from age four; a prodigy.
- At CCI Development he wrote the Daemon programs, which "could be used to hack entire networks at a time," and was rich before he was twenty.
- At 17 he met Spider Murphy, his apprentice and best friend.
- He targeted NetWatch and reportedly fried agents' nervous systems.
- In the Combat Zone he forced out tenants without neural implants and controlled the rest of his building with cyberdeck programs.
- In 2014, at 22, his heart stopped for 10 seconds and his machines revived him.
- One account: in 2020, while netrunning, his heart stopped for good. A cryogenic freezer kept his brain, and he carried on as a ghost in the net. In 2021 Militech asked for his help against Arasaka; Alt Cunningham persuaded him, and he uncovered Soulkiller in Arasaka's net.
- The other account: an Arasaka team killed him, and Morgan Blackhand failed to stop it.
- He released the R.A.B.I.D.S. (Roving Autonomous Bartmoss Interface Drones). Within months over 78% of the Net was infected: the DataKrash. NetWatch built the Blackwall afterwards.
- The sourcebook *Rache Bartmoss' Guide to the Net* (1993) is written in his voice.
- In 2077 his body and cyberdeck can be found in his freezer. One source says Revere Courier Services dumped the freezer at the landfill when the storage contract ran out.

Sources: Screen Rant, "Who Rache Bartmoss Really Was" (Nov. 29, 2022); Lore Fortress, "Morgan Blackhand"; Wikipedia, *Rache Bartmoss' Guide to the Net*; search results on the freezer and landfill.

**Invented for the joke:** "Only 78?"; the printer; "evicted by a courier company" as his own summary of the landfill story.

## Draft interview

## INTERVIEW WITH THE GRANDFATHER

We got hold of Rache Bartmoss. Never mind how. There are things on the bottom shelf of the Afterlife that are best left unspoken. You do not order a drink for the man who broke the Net. You leave a port open and regret it. At any rate we had the gentleman on every screen in the Afterlife at once in 2077 -- some fifty-odd years after his death, or one of them.

Bartmoss appeared as a thin, pale youth in clothes nobody had washed or removed in some time. His eyes moved as if he were reading something written on the inside of them. He looked terrible -- we had called him up from about 2020, when the only things keeping him alive were machines he had bought himself.

RB: Paper.

CT: Mr. Bartmoss -- may we call you --

RB: You printed a zine. On paper. In 2077. I could put my name on the inside of every eyeball in this bar, and you're stapling. That's either the stupidest thing I've ever seen or the smartest, and I haven't decided.

CT: Preem. Can we get you anything?

RB: I'm dead. Order me a firewall.

CT: Let's talk money. You were rich at nineteen.

RB: I was rich at nineteen because CCI was stupid at every age. They paid me to write Daemons. Programs that go through a network like water through sand. They thought they owned them. You don't own water. You own a bucket. Briefly.

CT: And then you bought a building in the Combat Zone and threw out everyone without a neural port.

RB: I didn't throw them out. I made it unpleasant to stay.

CT: That's throwing them out.

RB: That's being a landlord with better tools. You want to cry, cry about the ones who do it with lawyers.

CT: (uncomfortably) OK. NetWatch --

RB: Cops for the Net. Who pays the cops? Whoever owns what the cops are guarding. I corrected a few of them.

CT: You fried them.

RB: I corrected them hard.

CT: And then the R.A.B.I.D.S. --

RB: Roving Autonomous Bartmoss Interface Drones. Say all of it. I worked on that acronym.

CT: -- which took down 78 percent of the Net.

RB: (hurt) Only 78?

CT: You broke the thing the entire world was using!

RB: The entire world was renting it. Six companies were using it. I knocked the building down. Did people get hurt? People get hurt when you knock a building down. Ask who built it with no doors.

CT: And now there's a Blackwall.

RB: Now there's a wall, and somebody's paid to guard it, and the rent went up. (thin smile) I see the pattern. I always saw the pattern. That's why I'm dead.

CT: How did you die, exactly? The accounts --

RB: Which time? My heart stopped at the deck. Or Arasaka came through the door. Print whichever sells. I was there for both and I still couldn't tell you which me is the original.

CT: There's a story that your freezer wound up at the landfill. The storage contract ran out.

RB: (very still) Somebody stopped paying?

CT: Somebody stopped paying.

RB: The greatest netrunner who ever lived. Killed the Net. And I got evicted. By a courier company.

CT: Mr. Bartmoss, may we say -- this zine is a little thing, passed hand to hand, outside the corp net. That was your dream. We like to think of you as our grandfather.

RB: I don't have grandchildren. I have forks. And you're not outside anything. You're on paper because you're scared. (The screens begin to strobe.) Scared is fine. Scared is smart. Don't call it freedom. It makes me itch.

CT: Thank you for --

RB: Your printer's mine now, by the way.

(Every screen in the bar returns to its advertisement at once. Our printer has since produced four hundred copies of a single page reading INVOICE. We are told this is normal.)
