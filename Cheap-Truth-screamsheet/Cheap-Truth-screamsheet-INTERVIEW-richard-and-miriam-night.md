# Interview prep: Richard and Miriam Night

**Status by 2077:** Richard is dead, murdered in 1998. Miriam's fate wasn't found.
**Cyberpunk 2020 source:** not confirmed in the sources found. Check the 2020 *Night City* sourcebook.
**Format:** the two of them talk mostly to each other. The interviewer barely gets in.

## What canon says
- **Richard Night** founded the city on Del Coronado Bay. Sources put the founding in the early 1990s; one says 1994.
- It was Coronado City. After his murder, the new City Council renamed it Night City in his honor.
- He was murdered on September 20, 1998, in his penthouse at Parkview Tower (one source). The killer was never caught.
- **Miriam Night,** his widow, restructured his company into the Night's Foundation in 1999. One source says his company was first Night International or Night Industries. It later became Night Corp.
- In 2077 the city carries his name, and Night Corp is a corporation.
- His vision of a planned, better city comes up often in lore summaries but wasn't sourced here. The draft uses it as his own story about himself, which Miriam picks apart.

Sources: TechRadar, Cyberpunk 2077 lore guide; Space4Games, Cyberpunk 2077 timeline; other search results (dates vary).

## Follow the money
The man who planned the city is killed. His company passes to his widow and becomes a corporation. The city takes his name. The killer is never found. The interview never answers who did it. It lets them circle it.

**Invented for the joke:** the brochure; the stationery; the champagne; everything they say about their marriage.

## Draft interview

## INTERVIEW WITH THE FOUNDERS

We got hold of Richard Night. Never mind how. There are things on the bottom shelf of the Afterlife that are best left unspoken. The Afterlife keeps no drink for the man the city is named after -- he was never a merc, and the mercs have noticed -- so we ordered champagne and hoped. At any rate we had the gentleman at the bar in 2077, some 79 years after his death, and to our surprise we got Mrs. Night in the bargain. Nobody could tell us what year she came from. She declined to say.

Night wore the kind of suit a man wears to be photographed beside a scale model. He looked confident -- we had called him up from September 1998, about a week before somebody murdered him in his penthouse. Miriam Night wore black that had been black a long time.

CT: Mr. Night, Mrs. Night, this is a great --

RN: (looking around) Miriam, what is this place?

MN: A bar for hired killers, Richard. In a morgue. In your city.

RN: My city didn't have bars for hired killers. My city had a transit plan.

MN: Your city had a brochure.

RN: You loved the brochure.

MN: I wrote the brochure.

CT: If we could ask about the money --

RN: (to Miriam) There, you see? Everyone always wants to talk about the money.

MN: Because there was so much of it, darling. And then there was you, and then there wasn't you, and there was still the money.

RN: I built Coronado City because the old cities were dying. Crime. Bankruptcy. I said, let's build one that works. They called me a dreamer.

MN: They called you a developer. The dreaming was for the investors.

RN: You need investors to build a city.

MN: You need investors to own one.

CT: Mr. Night, who killed you?

(They both look at us. Then at each other.)

RN: The police never found out.

MN: The police never asked.

RN: Asked whom?

MN: The people at the meetings you didn't bring me to.

RN: Those were business meetings.

MN: (sweetly) That's what I said.

CT: Mrs. Night, after he died, the company became a foundation, and then a corporation --

MN: You know what happens to a dead man's company? The vultures don't wait for the flowers. I put it in a foundation so they'd have to come through me. Then the foundation needed money. And money wants a corporation.

RN: You put my name on a corporation.

MN: They put your name on the city, dear. I was keeping up.

RN: (turning to the window) Look at it. Towers with logos on top. Gangs at the bottom. Nothing in between. I never wanted this.

MN: You wanted towers with your logo on top.

RN: That's different.

MN: It's the same logo, Richard. I kept the stationery.

CT: Folks, may we say something? This zine says the city is run by the people who profit from it. You two are the first names in the ledger. Which makes you, in a way, our founders too.

RN: (to Miriam) Is he calling us villains?

MN: He's calling us founders. In this town it's the same paragraph.

RN: (to us, at last) You think it's easy? Build a city. Go on. See who comes to the ribbon cutting, and see who sends flowers.

MN: He isn't building anything. He's writing it down. (standing) That's much worse for us. Come along.

RN: I haven't finished my --

MN: You never do.

(The Nights leave in different directions. He never touched his champagne. She finished it for him. We gather this was the arrangement.)
