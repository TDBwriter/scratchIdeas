# Interview prep: Johnny Silverhand, as of Cyberpunk 2020 and RED

**Status by 2077:** dead. Killed in 2023 at Arasaka Tower. The versions differ: in *Firestorm*, Adam Smasher kills him; in 2077, Arasaka soulkills him.
**Called up from:** 2020, before the tower. He knows nothing after that: no Relic, no V, no 2077.
**Fits the department:** yes. He has a drink at the Afterlife.

## What canon says
- First appears in the 1988 Cyberpunk rulebook, in the short story and introductory adventure "Never Fade Away."
- A rockerboy. Frontman of Samurai. By 2013 the band had split and he had a solo career.
- A veteran of the Second Central American Conflict, where the US experimented on its own troops with combat drugs and risky cybernetics.
- His girlfriend Alt Cunningham, a genius programmer, was kidnapped by Arasaka, forced to build Soulkiller, and killed by it.
- The last book of the 2020 line, set in the Fourth Corporate War, has him killed in Arasaka Tower by Adam Smasher, who worked for Saburo Arasaka.
- In Cyberpunk RED he's a dead legend of the Time of the Red.
- In 2077, the Afterlife serves a Johnny Silverhand.

Sources: PC Gamer, "Johnny Silverhand's tragic rock star backstory"; Den of Geek, "Who is Johnny Silverhand?"; Lore Fortress, "Morgan Blackhand." The silver cyberarm is common knowledge across the series and not separately sourced here.

**Invented for the joke:** the price of the drink; "I am against the shirts"; the flyer joke.

## Draft interview

## INTERVIEW WITH THE CONSCIENCE

We got hold of Johnny Silverhand. Never mind how. There are things on the bottom shelf of the Afterlife that are best left unspoken. This one was easy: the Afterlife sells a Johnny Silverhand, and half the people who order it are tourists. At any rate we had the gentleman on the next stool in 2077 -- some 54 years after his death.

Silverhand wore shades indoors, at night, and a jacket that had been slept in by more than one person. A silver cyberarm lay on the bar like a guitar case. He looked terrific -- we had called him up from about 2020, when he was still alive, which is to say still furious.

CT: Mr. Silverhand -- may we call you Johnny? -- this is a great pleasure.

JS: (draining the glass in front of him) What is this?

CT: It's a Johnny Silverhand.

JS: (pleased) They named a drink after me?

CT: They name them after --

JS: After who?

CT: (uncomfortably) Legends.

JS: Damn right. (signals for another) Who gets the money?

CT: The bar.

JS: (the mood goes) Of course the bar.

CT: Let's talk about Samurai.

JS: Best thing I ever did, and I've been trying to get out from under it since. You start a band because you've got something to say. Then a label buys it and you've got something to sell. Then there's a contract that says how loud. You know who never got paid? The kids in the front row. They paid us. We paid the label. The label paid a corp. The corp put its name on the arena. I was singing against the corps in a building with their logo on the roof.

CT: Far out. And before the music, the war --

JS: (flexing the silver hand) Central America. Our own government ran us like lab rats. Combat drugs. Chrome nobody had tested on anyone who could sue. They sent kids down there to find out what kills kids. I came home with this and a list.

CT: A list?

JS: People who owe. Most of them run something now.

CT: And Alt --

JS: Don't.

CT: We only --

JS: Alt Cunningham was the smartest person in every room she walked into, including the ones I was in. Arasaka took her because she was the best, made her build the thing that killed her, and called it a product. You want to follow the money on that? You don't have to follow it. It's got a logo on the side.

CT: You know they still wear your face in this town? Shirts. Walls. There's a --

JS: Who's selling the shirts?

CT: (checking) It doesn't say.

JS: It never says. (signals for a third) I want it understood that I am against the shirts.

CT: Johnny, may we say something? You're more than a rockerboy to us. You're the city's conscience. Truth to power. This zine is carrying your torch --

JS: You sign it?

CT: No.

JS: You print it on what?

CT: Paper.

JS: (laughs wildly) I played with my face forty feet high and the amps past legal. You're passing notes in class! "Carrying my torch" -- kid, you're not even the opening act. You're the guy handing out flyers for the opening act. (wiping his eyes) Conscience. Jesus. I'm a guy with a guitar and a grudge, and the grudge is bigger. Put that on a shirt.

CT: We'd rather not sell shirts.

JS: (delighted) Good answer. Who's paying for these?

(Silverhand winks out before the bill arrives. Three Johnny Silverhands, 54 eddies. We did not tell him about 2023. Nobody tells you about your last show.)
