# mobileLaundryBus

*Status: brainstorming. A renovated short bus as a mobile wash-and-fold and dry-cleaning service.*

## The concept

Take a short bus (a "shorty" — a 14–20 ft cutaway school/shuttle bus, ~4–8 window), gut it, and build it out as a rolling laundry service. It parks at apartment complexes, office lots, gyms, campuses, farmers' markets, and events, or runs a scheduled neighborhood route, offering wash-and-fold and dry-cleaning pickup/delivery. The pitch to customers: laundry done for you, where you are, without a trip to a laundromat or cleaner.

## The one hard truth up front

**You can't really dry-clean on a bus.** True dry-cleaning uses solvents — perchloroethylene (PERC) is a hazardous air pollutant with heavy EPA and state controls, and even the greener alternatives (hydrocarbon, GreenEarth/silicone, liquid CO₂) need large, fixed, permitted machines with vapor recovery and waste handling. None of that is street-legal or safe in a moving vehicle. So the realistic split is:

- **Onboard (or pickup/delivery):** wash-and-fold — the volume business.
- **Hub-and-spoke:** dry-cleaning is *collected* by the bus and processed at a fixed plant — either your own later, or a wholesale partner cleaner now. The bus is the storefront and logistics; the plant is where solvents live.

Framing the bus as a **mobile front counter + wash-and-fold workshop**, not a mobile dry-cleaner, is what keeps this buildable and legal.

## Two operating models (pick one to start, or blend)

1. **Wash-on-board.** Commercial washer/dryers run inside the bus while parked. Fast turnaround, visible, a draw. But it demands serious water, power, heat, and wastewater capacity (see below) and limits how many loads per stop.
2. **Pickup & delivery (logistics-only).** The bus is a routed collection/drop vehicle; washing happens at a small fixed base or a partner laundromat. Far cheaper to build, scales better, but less of a spectacle and dependent on base capacity.

A sensible path: **start logistics-only, add a small wash-on-board capability once routes prove out.**

## The build-out (wash-on-board version)

- **Fresh water:** 40–100 gal onboard tank; refill at the base or via spigot. Commercial washers are thirsty (~15–30+ gal/load), so water is the binding constraint on loads-per-stop.
- **Water heating:** on-demand propane or electric water heater — hot water is most of the job.
- **Graywater:** a holding tank **at least as large as fresh** (you can't discharge laundry wastewater to the street or a storm drain — it needs to go to an approved sanitary/sewer disposal point). This is a legal and logistical pillar, not an afterthought.
- **Power:** commercial dryers and heaters are the load. Options: a quiet inverter generator (propane/diesel), a large battery + inverter bank with shore-power top-up, or shore power when a venue provides a 30/50A hookup. Dryers are the hardest part to power off-grid — propane dryers sidestep much of it.
- **Equipment:** 1–2 commercial/high-efficiency washers + propane dryers, a folding station, shelving/bins, a counter, a POS tablet.
- **Ventilation & moisture:** dryer venting, exhaust, and humidity control so the bus doesn't become a mold box.
- **Chassis:** the short bus itself — buy used (ex-school/shuttle, ~$5–15k running), inspect the drivetrain, and plan the floor/electrical/plumbing retrofit around payload and weight limits (water is ~8.3 lb/gal — 100 gal fresh + 100 gal gray is ~1,700 lb before equipment).

## Services & add-ons

- Wash-and-fold by the pound (the core).
- Dry-cleaning pickup/delivery (hub-and-spoke).
- Delicates / hang-dry, folding tiers, same-day at events.
- Subscriptions (weekly household bag), commercial accounts (gyms = towels, salons, Airbnb turnover, restaurants = aprons/linens — recurring B2B is the steadiest money).
- Eco angle: high-efficiency machines, measured water use, fragrance-free/hypoallergenic options.

## Target customers & siting

- **Residential density without in-unit laundry:** apartment/condo complexes, student housing — park on a schedule.
- **Busy-professional convenience:** office parks, hospitals (shift workers), gyms.
- **B2B recurring:** gyms, salons, short-term rentals, small restaurants, pet groomers.
- **Events/markets:** visibility and one-off volume.

Siting means **permission to park** — property-manager agreements, lot-use deals, event permits. No permission, no customers; locking in a few anchor complexes or B2B accounts is the real first milestone.

## Money (rough, to be grounded with local numbers)

- **Startup:** used bus $5–15k; build-out $15–50k+ depending on wash-on-board vs logistics-only (tanks, machines, power, plumbing, finish); permits/insurance/branding on top. Logistics-only can start well under half of a full wash-on-board build.
- **Revenue:** wash-and-fold commonly $1.75–3.00+/lb; dry-clean markup over wholesale; subscription and B2B contracts for baseline cash flow.
- **Unit economics to watch:** loads per stop (water-bound), stops per day (route density), labor per load, fuel, and the fixed drag of insurance and permits. B2B contracts smooth the lumpiness of retail.

## Regulatory & practical checklist (verify locally — this varies a lot by state/county/city)

- **Business:** LLC, business license, sales tax registration.
- **Vehicle:** commercial registration; whether a CDL is needed (usually not for a short bus under 26,001 lb GVWR, but confirm with the built-out weight); commercial auto insurance.
- **Wastewater:** the big one — lawful collection, holding, and disposal of laundry graywater to an approved sanitary point; likely health-department and/or wastewater-authority rules.
- **Water:** potable-water handling if you advertise clean/sanitary service.
- **Propane/generator:** fuel storage and appliance codes, fire safety, CO detection.
- **Dry-cleaning:** if you ever operate your own plant, environmental permits for solvents (PERC is tightly controlled; wet-cleaning and hydrocarbon are the more permit-friendly routes).
- **Parking/vending:** mobile-vendor permits and the private-property agreements above.
- **Commercial kitchen-style scrutiny:** some jurisdictions treat mobile service vehicles like food trucks for inspection — expect a plan-review process.

## Risks

- **Wastewater disposal** being harder/costlier than expected (the quiet killer of mobile laundry).
- **Power/water limits** capping loads-per-stop below break-even for wash-on-board.
- **Parking access** — losing an anchor site can gut a route.
- **Weather/seasonality**, breakdowns (bus + machines = two things that fail), and the reality that one vehicle is a single point of failure.
- **Regulatory surprise** in any of the checklist items stalling launch.

## Premium protocol: concierge garment care

*The high-margin tier for affluent, dense neighborhoods (big houses, busy families, expensive clothes). These customers aren't paying for pounds washed; they're paying for sorting, stain handling, and making garments last.*

**The science behind it.** Cleaning comes from four interchangeable factors — **Temperature, Agitation, Chemistry, Time** (Sinner's Circle). Standard laundry turns all four up for every load. The premium protocol gives each load only what its soil and fabric need. That cleans better *and* is gentler on clothes, and it's the same discipline that keeps the bus's water and power budget in check.

### How it works

1. **Intake & tagging.** Each household gets bags by category (or we sort at pickup). Garments are tagged per family member. Stains are flagged on a card at handoff (what it is and when it happened), because the stain type sets the treatment.
2. **Fine sort by soil × fabric**, not just lights and darks.
3. **Stain triage before any wash**, using the protocols below.
4. **Category-specific cycles** (table below).
5. **Inspect before drying.** Dryer heat sets stains permanently, so nothing goes in the dryer until its stain is confirmed gone.
6. **Finish:** fold, hang, or press per garment; returned by person and category.

### Load categories (a typical family of four with pets: about 13 loads a week)

| Category | Wash | Chemistry | Dry |
|---|---|---|---|
| Workwear (canvas, denim, work cotton) | Pre-soak, 40–60 °C, heavy-duty, extra rinse | Heavy-duty enzymatic + degreaser | Medium tumble |
| Dress shirts / blouses | Pretreat collars and cuffs, 30–40 °C, low spin | Regular enzymatic | Hang / low, press |
| Wool, silk, suits | Wet-clean or wool cycle, cold (or route to a dry-clean partner) | **Protease-free** wool detergent (proteases digest protein fibers) | Flat / professional |
| Performance/athletic wear | Inside out, cold–30 °C, max 40 °C, gentle | Sport detergent; **no softener** | **Air dry** (heat breaks down elastane) |
| Kids' everyday (teen odor, grass, food) | 30–40 °C; delicates in mesh bags on cold | Enzymatic + oxygen booster for teen odor | Low; air dry delicates |
| Underwear & socks | Cotton 60 °C; elastane 40 °C + oxygen bleach | Enzymatic | Medium |
| Bath towels | 60 °C, extra rinse | Enzymatic + oxygen bleach; **no softener** | Medium–high |
| Kitchen linens | 60 °C, kept apart from bath towels | Heavy-duty + oxygen bleach | Tumble |
| Sheets & bedding | 60 °C (kills dust mites, reduces pet dander); silk on cold | Enzymatic | Medium / line |
| Pet bedding & throws | De-hair first, 60 °C, run alone, extra rinse | **Fragrance-free** enzymatic | Tumble; clean the drum afterward |
| Darks & prized colors | Inside out, cold, gentle | Color-safe, no optical brighteners | Line / low |

### Stain triage (the core skill customers pay for)

- **Blood / protein** (nosebleed, scraped knee, kitchen cut): **cold water only**, because heat cooks protein into the fiber. Flush from the back, apply an enzymatic (protease) pretreat, and on colorfast whites use 3% peroxide after a spot test. Wool and silk get no protease: cold water plus wool wash, or route to the partner.
- **Oil / grease** (food, cooking): blot, draw the oil out with cornstarch or talc, work in dish soap or a solvent pretreat, then wash in the **warmest water the fabric allows** with lipase. Oil is the opposite of blood: heat helps.
- **Tannin / wine / coffee:** blot, cold flush from the back, **no heat and no true soap** early on (both set tannins), then an oxygen-bleach (percarbonate) soak if the fabric is colorfast.
- **Grass / mud:** let mud dry and brush it off first; treat grass with enzymes before washing.

### Pet add-on

De-hair before washing (a 10-minute no-heat tumble plus a lint pass), use fragrance-free chemistry, add an extra rinse, and run pet loads alone with a drum and filter clean afterward. Households with a dog and a cat will pay for this on its own.

### Pricing logic

- Charge a **per-bag or per-household subscription**, not per-pound. The value is the sorting and handling, which pounds don't capture.
- **Stain treatment** is included up to a set number of flagged stains per week, then billed per item.
- **Pressing, wool/silk, and pet bedding** are add-ons. Dry-clean-only items route to the partner at a markup.
- A **per-garment care record** (fabric, past stains, preferences) is the retention moat: once we know a family's wardrobe, switching services costs them that knowledge.

### Operational implications

- The tier runs on **many small, specialized loads**. That fits a fixed base or partner facility with several machines better than one onboard washer. **The bus does premium pickup, intake, and delivery; the base does the processing.**
- It needs more detergent types on hand (heavy-duty, regular enzymatic, protease-free wool, sport, fragrance-free, color-safe, oxygen bleach) and staff trained in stain triage.
- Only about a third of loads need 60 °C; the rest run cold or warm, which keeps energy use modest despite the higher load count.

## Phased rollout

1. **Prove demand, logistics-only.** Buy the bus, brand it, run pickup/delivery with washing at a partner laundromat or small base. Land 2–3 anchor B2B accounts and a subscription base. Cheapest way to learn the routes and the numbers.
2. **Add wash-on-board** once routes justify it — start with one washer + propane dryer and modest tanks; grow capacity as demand proves out.
3. **Add owned dry-cleaning** (optional, much later) only if volume justifies a fixed plant; keep the bus as the mobile front end.

## Decisions for you

1. **Wash-on-board vs logistics-only to start?** (Recommend logistics-only first.)
2. **Retail (per-pound households) vs B2B contracts (gyms/rentals/restaurants) as the primary customer?** (Recommend B2B for cash-flow stability, retail as the visible draw.)
3. **Dry-cleaning now (partner wholesaler) or skip until later?**
4. **Service area & anchor sites** — which complexes/accounts to lock first.
5. **Power path** — propane-forward (dryers + heat) vs battery/shore-power.
6. **Lead with the premium concierge tier?** It has higher margin and steadier subscriptions in affluent neighborhoods, but it needs a base with several machines and trained stain-triage staff. (Recommend piloting it with a handful of households alongside basic wash-and-fold.)
