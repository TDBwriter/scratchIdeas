# Mission: Professionals for a Level Playing Field

**Purpose:** Train professionals in civic service, education, the military, healthcare, and science and research, in a safe and stable environment, and export that expertise worldwide to bring all of humanity to a level playing field.

Every other file in this set serves this purpose:

| File | Role |
|---|---|

| `infrastructureHubValidation.md` | Engineering and consistency check of the blueprint |
| `infrastructureHubRecommendations.md` | Viability fixes: the reactor complex, the re-laid stack, and safeguards |
| `infrastructureHubPortAuPrince.md` | Site adaptation for Port-au-Prince |
| `infrastructureHubSolarMobility.md` | Solar, batteries, the electric fleet, and the donated-capital model |

| `infrastructureHubMission.md` | This file: the purpose, the professional tracks, and the export model |

**Bottom line:**
- **The hub is a teaching city.** Its infrastructure is the classroom, the way a teaching hospital is both a hospital and a school. Every station, clinic, kiosk, solar canopy, and reactor is a training site, and every Community Team is both a workforce and an apprenticeship.
- **The central risk is brain drain.** Historically, 80–84% of Haiti's college graduates have lived abroad. Haiti is also the only country in the Americas on the WHO's health-workforce safeguards list, which flags countries that recruiters shouldn't actively hire health workers away from. If "export" means exporting people, the hub hollows out the place it is building. So **the hub exports knowledge, institutions, and rotating teams, and its people come home.**
- **Haiti can already produce internationally accredited professionals.** The emergency medicine residency at the University Hospital of Mirebalais earned international (ACGME-I) accreditation in 2024. **The same hospital closed in April 2025** after gangs stormed the town. Safety is the precondition for the whole mission.

## 1. The professional tracks

| Track | Trains on | Where | What Haiti can teach the world |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Civic** (public administration, utility operation, regulators including the nuclear regulator, planners, judges, prosecutors, legal aid, governance) | Stations, utilities, the Processing Yard, courts, the Multi-Key Council institutions | City Campus | Running a city through crisis; governance that resists capture; sanitation without sewers; public accountability for the use of force |
| **Education** (teachers, apprenticeship instructors, trainers of trainers) | Schools, resource centers, Community Teams | City Campus and stations | Teaching in the local language (Kreyòl first) for underserved language communities; teaching in disrupted settings |
| **Military and security** | **A separate Security Academy under state authority** (see below) | A separate site | The enemy-led-escalation doctrine: stabilization with restraint, proven by public data. This expertise is rare anywhere in the world. |
| **Healthcare** (physicians and residents, nurses, community health workers, radiotherapy physicists, nuclear medicine) | The Base Hospital as a teaching hospital, and the station clinics | City Campus and clinics | Health systems on limited resources; cholera control; trauma care; community health worker models |
| **Science, research, and engineering** (reactor operators, neutron science, seismology, earthquake and hurricane engineering, solar and battery, water and sanitation, public health) | The research reactor, the active fault, the solar and battery lab, the Processing Yard | North Campus and City Campus | Disaster resilience and tropical infrastructure, studied in a living lab |
| **Library and data science** (librarians, archivists, data curators, machine-learning engineers, AI evaluators, Kreyòl linguists) | The Level -1 library and its evaluation cluster | North Campus and City Campus | Building lawful, open collections and AI evaluations for low-resource languages |

**The military track keeps the firewall.** The hub funds open academic courses that officers can take like anyone else: law of armed conflict, human rights law, de-escalation, civilian-harm analysis, trauma medicine, and engineering. **Weapons and tactical training are funded by the state and UN** at a separate academy. No hub money, vehicles, or data go to armed units, and Community Team members never enter the military track while they serve.

## 2. The teaching city: how training is built in

- **Every site is a classroom:**
  - **Community Teams** are apprenticeships.
  - **The Processing Yard** teaches sanitation engineering.
  - **The solar and battery lab** teaches technicians.
  - **The Base Hospital** runs residencies.
  - **The research reactor** trains reactor operators and nuclear scientists.
  - **The ledger** teaches data science, under the privacy firewall.
- **A career ladder:** apprentice, then technician, then professional, then faculty. This is the blueprint's own pipeline: Apprenticeships ➔ Technocrats ➔ Faculty Leaders. Each Community Team role leads to a degree path.
- **Free tuition, paid for by the grant and the endowment, in exchange for a period of service at home** equal to the years of free study.
  - Service is paid at competitive local salaries funded by the endowment.
  - A graduate can buy out the commitment at cost.
  - People stay because of pay and purpose, not compulsion.
- **International accreditation for every program.** If degrees aren't recognized abroad, the export path fails. The Mirebalais emergency medicine residency is the template.
- **Teaching languages:** Kreyòl, French, English, and Spanish.
- **Research ethics:**
  - An independent ethics board.
  - Consent in Kreyòl.
  - Research data falls under the same firewall, so none of it ever goes to Response Teams.

## 3. Exporting expertise without draining Haiti

**The rule: export knowledge, institutions, and rotating teams, not a one-way flow of people.**

1. **Open-source everything.** Curricula, designs (station canopies, swap cabinets, sanitation containers), operating manuals, and governance code are all openly licensed. This takes the blueprint's "Open-Source Proliferation" mandate global.
2. **Export Teams.** The Community Team format becomes the export unit. A 12-person team deploys to a partner city for a fixed rotation, stands up services, trains local successors, and comes home. It is the same Green Beret logic, applied in reverse: work through local people and leave them in charge.
3. **Replication kits.** The Phase 0–6 build sequence is adapted to each new site. The second hub in Haiti comes first, then sites abroad, starting with places in similar conditions: small island states, conflict-affected cities, and cities without sewers.
4. **Partner students.** Students from partner countries train in Haiti and take the model home.
5. **Guardrails for people who do go abroad:**
   - **Voluntary, time-limited, and fully paid to the professional,** with no state or hub cut of salaries. **The counter-example:** in 2019, UN special rapporteurs warned that conditions in Cuba's medical missions abroad "could amount to forced labour."
   - **Follow the WHO Global Code of Practice on the international recruitment of health personnel.** Because Haiti is on the safeguards list, partners don't actively recruit its health workers, and every partnership spells out what Haiti gains.
   - **Fellowships for the diaspora.** Haitian professionals abroad come home for teaching terms. The diaspora becomes faculty, which reverses the drain.
   - **Partner fees cover cost only and go to the endowment.** There is no profit motive.

**What "a level playing field" means in practice:** success is the number of places that can run their own hubs **without needing Haiti's help.**

## 4. Safety and stability come first

- **Mirebalais is the warning.** A hospital that produced internationally accredited physicians closed when its town fell. Every campus has to sit inside a secured area from the start.
- **Campuses are civic spaces.** Under the stabilization model, Response Teams guard only the perimeters of critical infrastructure, such as the North Campus reactor complex. The City Campus relies on its location inside the secured area and on Haitian National Police community policing, never on armed teams inside.
- **Training continues if a campus closes.** Distance learning runs over the Level -1 network, and satellite classrooms operate at stations. One site falling must not stop the pipeline.

## 5. Measures of success

| Measure | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Graduates per year, by track | Output |
| **Share of graduates working in Haiti 5 and 10 years later** | Directly measures progress against the historical 80% loss |
| Number of internationally accredited programs | Whether the qualifications travel |
| Export rotations completed, and returned | Whether the export model works without draining Haiti |
| **Partner sites running independently** | The real measure of a level playing field |
| Diaspora professionals returning to teach | Whether the drain is reversing |
| Openly published research, designs, and curricula | Whether knowledge is actually shared |

## 6. Changes to the build order

- **Phase 1:** the College starts in distributed form from day one: apprenticeships through Community Teams, teacher training, and community health worker training. (Before, the College came in Phase 3.)
- **Phase 3:** the City Campus adds degree programs, the teaching hospital, and residencies.
- **Phase 4:** the research reactor adds nuclear science and operator training.
- **Phase 6:** the second Haitian hub becomes the **first export,** built by Haitian Export Teams. International partner sites follow.
