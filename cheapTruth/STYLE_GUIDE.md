# CHEAP TRUTH — Voice & Pattern Guide

V's screamsheet. One issue per mission, published after that mission is complete. Every issue stands alone, so it stays accurate no matter what order the missions are played in.

Modeled on the original *Cheap Truth* (Austin, Texas, 1983–1986), edited by "Vincent Omniaveritas" (Bruce Sterling): free, photocopied, **NOT COPYRIGHTED**. Male V's full name in the game is Vincent, which gives the title a second layer.

---

## 1. The pattern

Every issue has these parts, in this order.

| # | Part | Original *Cheap Truth* | *Cheap Truth* 2077 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Masthead** | "CHEAP TRUTH" plus the issue number, redrawn every issue in a different display face (angled rub-down letters, stencil, outline, op-art, stacked vertically, boxed letters) | Same: redrawn for every mission. The issue ID is part of the masthead. |
| 2 | **EDITORIAL.** | Opens nearly every issue. 1–5 sentences in a direct voice that set the issue's conceit, sometimes with a quoted tagline. *"Hi. You want to know the truth. We want to tell it to you."* | Opens with **EDITORIAL.** V's direct, first-person voice. |
| 3 | **Lead feature** | An ALL-CAPS headline (a pun, tabloid style, or a mock news headline), then the piece | The mission as V lived and documented it |
| 4 | **Departments** (0–2) | Recurring: **CHEAP TRUTH TOP TEN** (numbered capsules), **INTERVIEW** (Q&A, sometimes with the dead), **GRIPE TIME**, **BULLETIN**, correspondent dispatches | Pick what suits the mission (see §3) |
| 5 | **Clip art** | Black-and-white found images (engravings, technical diagrams, op-art, silhouettes) dropped into gaps, often running off the edge | Photo-mode shots treated as found images |
| 6 | **Colophon** | A fixed formula, a new slogan each issue (see §4) | Same formula, V's details |

**Length:** the originals ran about 1,200–2,000 words, typically about 1,500, on one sheet printed both sides.

## 2. Voice

**From the original**
- **Direct address.** It talks to the reader: *"You want to know the truth."*
- **Mock-serious conceits** to open. Issue #3 claims black marketeers are selling *Cheap Truth* "at astronomical prices, some going as high as twelve to thirteen cents."
- **Blunt verdicts.** Capsules end in one hard line: *"Makes more sense than the movie."*
- **Vivid creature metaphors.** *"As American SF lies in a reptilian torpor, its small, squishy cousin, Fantasy, creeps gecko-like across the bookstands."*
- **Tabloid headlines.** *"SF WRITER EATS OWN FOOT TO SURVIVE!"* · *"PUBLIC SHUDDERS AT 'BEST OF THE YEAR'"*
- **A one-line closer** that ends a piece: *"Physician, heal thyself."*
- **Contributors with epithets**, introduced fresh each time: *"CHEAP TRUTH stalwart Sue Denim sharpens her lance and charges the windmill."* · *"guest agitatrix Hunilla de Cholo."*
- **A target.** The original went after the science fiction establishment. *Cheap Truth* 2077's target: ______________

**From V** (see the "About Me" draft)
- First person. Streetkid out of Heywood, now in a cube in Watson.
- Slang: *choom, choomba, eddies, chrome/chromed, output, virtu, flatline, gonk.*
- Profanity is fine.
- Shout-outs to the crew.
- Signs off as **Veritas**.

---

## 3. Departments

Use none, one or two per issue.

- **CHEAP TRUTH TOP TEN**: numbered capsules on the people, places, gear and deals from this mission.
  `3. VIKTOR VEKTOR. Ripperdoc, Little China. Steady hands, soft heart. Owed: 21,000 eddies.`
- **INTERVIEW**: a Q&A with someone from the mission. The original ran mock interviews (Lovecraft, Raymond Chandler) and ended its run interviewing its own editor.
- **BULLETIN** / **GRIPE TIME**: short items, rumors, complaints.
- **DISPATCH**: a piece credited to a crew member under an epithet (Jackie, T-Bug).

## 4. Colophon

Original formula:

> CHEAP TRUTH, 809-C West 12th Street, Austin, Texas 78701. Vincent Omniaveritas, editing. Todd Refinery, graphics. NOT COPYRIGHTED. "Nothing Better To Do"

*Cheap Truth* 2077 formula:

> CHEAP TRUTH, [in-world address]. Veritas, editing. [name], graphics. NOT COPYRIGHTED. "[slogan for this issue]"
> Veritas – [timestamp, in your draft's format]

Slogans from the original, for reference:
"Xerox pirates, start your engines!" · "SERVING SF THROUGH SAMIZDAT" · "Xerox pirates, to the barricades!" · "Nothing Better To Do" · "The Truth Cannot Be Copyrighted" · "It is better to do something than to be someone" · "Get Nineties!" · "Bored with the Apocalypse" · "The More Things Stay The Same, The More They Change" · "Where Mutation Is the Norm" · "Smugglers in the Marketplace of Ideas" · "Think globally, act locally" · "When the going gets weird, the weird turn pro" · "Ars Longa, Vita Brevis"

## 5. Standalone rules

These keep each issue accurate whatever the playing order.

1. **The issue ID belongs to the mission, not to the publishing order.** Use the mission's fixed number (for example, its entry number in the Pre-Heist Planning Log), never "the 12th issue we've put out."
2. **Only write what this mission guarantees.** Check the mission's *Requires* in the planning log. For example, V's Kiroshi eyes only exist from The Ripperdoc on; T-Bug is only known from Practice Makes Perfect or The Rescue on.
3. **No references to other issues.** No "last issue," "next time," "as we reported," and no running counts ("our third gig this week").
4. **No running totals.** Leave out Street Cred, V's level, and V's total eddies. Report only this mission's own payout.
5. **Introduce people every time**, with a short epithet, as the original did. Any issue may be someone's first.
6. **The timestamp is when the mission was completed,** in the world's time (the game is set in 2077).
7. **Report what actually happened in this playthrough**: the choices made, the route taken, who walked away.
