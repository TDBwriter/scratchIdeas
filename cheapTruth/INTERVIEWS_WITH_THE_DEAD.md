# CHEAP TRUTH: Interviews with the Dead

An optional department. A dead (or presumed dead) Cyberpunk 2020 legend is called up and interviewed. It counts as one of an issue's 0-2 optional departments (STYLE_GUIDE §3).

---

## What the original did

Checked against the scans. The original ran three of these.

- **#3, "INTERVIEW WITH THE MARTYR":** H. P. Lovecraft, "called up from about 1935." He's offered coffee: "With five sugars, please." He talks money: "You can't make a businessman out of a corpse." He rages at the editor who "starved us, cheated us" and set his stories "cheek by jowl with truss ads." The interviewer hails him as a martyr ("It was the act of a saint"), and Lovecraft answers with bigotry. The interview ends fast.
- **#11, the Raymond Chandler interview:** Chandler "arriving from 1957," in an ivory linen suit, orders "A Ballantine's on the rocks." He sneers at MTV and talks pulp pay rates. When the interviewer claims him as a fellow genre writer, Chandler laughs at science fiction ("You call that writing?") and "winks out with a crackle of static."
- **#17, "INTERVIEW WITH VINCENT OMNIAVERITAS":** a "post-mortem interview" with the zine's own editor, who turns out to be alive: "Heard you were dead." / "Not a scratch on me."
- **The summoning:** a running joke, "the unspeakable necromancy of the Cross Plains Dairy Queen."

**The pattern**
1. **The summoning:** the running ritual, the date, and the year of the dead person's life they're "called up from."
2. **How they look** in that year.
3. **The drink they order**, which tells you who they are.
4. **Money and work:** what they were paid, and who cheated them.
5. **The zine claims them as its own, and they refuse.** The joke cuts at the hero and at the zine.
6. **A sudden exit.**

**Corrections to the pasted summary**
- The dead don't endorse the zine. Both Lovecraft and Chandler turn on it.
- Sturgeon's issue is a eulogy by Brian Aldiss, not an interview.
- No Philip K. Dick interview turned up in the scans.

## *Cheap Truth* 2077: the department

**The summoning: the Afterlife.** Night City's merc bar names its drinks after legends, and the drinks honor the dead. Since Update 1.5 there's even a Jackie Welles. In the zine, ordering a legend's drink at the Afterlife calls the legend up. Morgan Blackhand has no drink there, because nobody is sure he's dead, so he shows up anyway, the way Omniaveritas did in #17.

**Rules**
1. **Who:** a Cyberpunk 2020 character who is dead, or believed dead, by 2077, and who is part of 2077's world. Never the crew: Jackie has a drink, but the crew is never named.
2. **Tie to this mission (E1).** Name the tie.
3. **Canon only (D4).** What they did and how they died, as the sources agree. Where the sources disagree, the interview can play with the disagreement instead of picking a side.
4. **Follow the money (§0).** Who profited from their life, and from their death.
5. **The dead refuse to be claimed.** The zine tries to make them its ancestor; they won't have it. The joke cuts both ways, and still never at the street (§2).
6. **The voice.** The "I" asks the questions and the dead answer in their own voices. Nothing only the crew could know.
7. **The images (G1):** the drink on the bar, the empty stool. Ordering at the Afterlife only becomes possible once The Heist starts.
8. **Stands alone (§5).** Introduce the legend fresh every time.

### Real people
- **The conflict:** the Pondsmith and CD Projekt pieces name living, real people. That breaks test F1 and §7 ("real events are never named"), and they can't be read as Night City reporting.
- **Ways to run them:**
  - outside the main-job issues, as a special, the way the original ran its Sturgeon issue;
  - as a named exception to F1 and §7;
  - or not at all.
- **Labelled parody.** Both open with a line saying the interview never happened and the person didn't say it.
- **Keep them to opinion and caricature.** Pondsmith's lines stay close to what he's said on the record: Cyberpunk as "a warning, not an aspiration," "inherently political," "don't preach." I found nothing on the record from him about censorship itself. The CD Projekt piece makes no factual claims about the three men's conduct; the satire is the economic theory.
- **"Kiciński" is ambiguous:** Michał (co-founder) or Adam (longtime joint CEO). The draft uses the surname only.
