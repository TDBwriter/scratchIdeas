## WHO GOT PAID FOR A CAR THAT NEVER MOVED?

**EDITORIAL.** Hi. You want to know who got paid for a car that never left its parking space. So do I. I followed one bartender's debt from a stool in Heywood to a garage under Embers, and I counted who walked out ahead. This costs nothing. Copy it and pass it on.

---

## ARASAKA MAN KEEPS HIS CAR; TWO THIEVES KEEP THEIR HEADACHES
*(Heywood / Embers)*

I watched an Arasaka man keep his car. Kaoru Fujioka, corp, still has his Rayfield Aerondight. Two would-be thieves have headaches. Everybody else went home with exactly what they came with, and in Night City, choom, that's a fairy tale.

It started where Heywood always starts: a bar and a debt. I was at El Coyote Cojo when I heard it. Pepe, the man pouring, owes Kirk. Due tomorrow. Nobody in that room asked how a bartender sinks that deep with a man like Kirk. In Heywood you don't ask how. You ask when.

Somebody at the bar offered to help. I watched them climb the stairs to Kirk, the creditor who holds court up there with Big Joe, and word came down ahead of them: lift an Aerondight from the garage under Embers and Pepe's slate is clean. Kirk even supplied the tool, a little box that sweet-talks the car's security into saying yes. Generous. A man who holds your debt and then hands you the means to pay it owns you twice.

The ride over belonged to Padre, the Heywood fixer, with Marcus Barros along for it. I watched a 6th Street crew pull that car over and lean on Padre. Then they let it go. In Heywood, that's a toll booth.

Under Embers I watched a man named Rick, the one in the security booth, sell twenty minutes. Twenty minutes of dead cameras. That's what a security system is worth when the man watching it needs eddies too.

The box worked. The car opened. It didn't matter. Someone else was already there for the same car on the same night, gun out. Two thieves, one Aerondight, zero plan for that.

Then the NCPD settled it. Cuffs on both. I watched the owner have a word with the officers, and I watched one officer end the conversation the short way. Both thieves woke up on the pavement. Unpaid. Still breathing.

**How it came to be.** A bartender's debt, passed down to whoever was dumb or kind enough to carry it. A creditor who'd rather collect in favors than in eddies, and lets the debtor's friends do the lifting.

**Who profited.** Before: Kirk, holding paper on a man who can't pay. After: Kaoru Fujioka, who kept his car, had a word with the cops, and lost nothing. Rick, for twenty minutes of looking away. Did Kirk get his money? Nobody's telling me.

**Who paid.** Two kids off the street, who did the dangerous part for somebody else's debt and walked off with nothing but bruises. I've watched that deal get offered to everyone without a corp badge: carry the debt, take the beating, be grateful you woke up.

Nobody got the car. The two thieves got each other. Some partnerships start with a handshake. I watched this one start at gunpoint.

## CHEAP TRUTH TOP TEN
*Heywood to Embers, as I saw it.*

1. **EL COYOTE COJO.** Heywood bar. Where the debt got poured. Cold drinks, warm trouble.
2. **PEPE.** Bartender. Owes Kirk, due tomorrow. The only honest man I saw all night, and the one holding the debt.
3. **KIRK.** Creditor, upstairs at the bar. Hands you the debt and the crowbar. Avoid.
4. **BIG JOE.** Keeps Kirk company upstairs. Big name. I'll leave it there.
5. **PADRE.** Heywood fixer. Gave the ride, took the heat from 6th Street. Worth knowing.
6. **MARCUS BARROS.** Rode along with Padre. Nobody leaned on him. Lucky or connected.
7. **6TH STREET.** A roadblock with opinions. Took nothing this time.
8. **RICK.** Security booth, Embers. Sells twenty minutes of blindness. Fair price, bad security.
9. **THE RAYFIELD AERONDIGHT.** The car everyone wanted and nobody got. Owned by an Arasaka man, kept with the NCPD's help.
10. **THE NCPD.** Cuffed two thieves, took the owner's word, put two kids on the pavement. Protect and serve. Serve whom?

## GRIPE TIME: DUE TOMORROW
Two words run Heywood: *due tomorrow*. Not a figure, not a rate. A deadline. I've watched the man who sets it, and he never carries anything heavier than a drink. The bartender who owes it can't leave his own bar. And the kids who pay it off don't owe anybody a single eddy. They just happened to be standing close when the bill came around. Somewhere upstairs a creditor is waiting to hear how it went, and he'll be fine either way. He always is.
