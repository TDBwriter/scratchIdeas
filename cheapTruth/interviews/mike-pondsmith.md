# Interview prep: Mike Pondsmith, on censorship

**Status:** a living, real person: the creator of Cyberpunk.
**Fits the department:** no. He isn't dead, and he isn't in-world.
**Rule conflicts:** F1 (no real-world person named) and §7 (real people unnamed). See `../INTERVIEWS_WITH_THE_DEAD.md`, "Real people."

## What's on the record
- Created Cyberpunk, a tabletop RPG, in 1988. Founder of R. Talsorian Games. Worked alongside CD Projekt Red on *Cyberpunk 2077*.
- Has said in several interviews that Cyberpunk is "a warning, not an aspiration": NME, Stevivor, the BBC, and a 2018 Q&A in *Edge*. In a later statement on police brutality he described it as a warning of what happens when power, money and ruthlessness combine.
- Has called Cyberpunk "inherently political," but not in the left-and-right sense (VGC). "Everything is political" (PC Gamer).
- On persuading people: "if you want to get somebody to kind of see your point of view, don't preach" (PCGamesInsider headline).
- Cyberpunk 2020's tenets: style over substance, attitude is everything, always take it to the edge, break the rules.
- **No statement of his on censorship itself turned up.** The draft keeps his lines to what he's said on the record (the warning, politics, not preaching), applied to censorship, and labels the piece as parody.

Sources: NME, "Cyberpunk is a warning, not an aspiration"; Stevivor; PC Gamer, "everything is political"; VGC interview; PCGamesInsider; *Edge* Q&A (PressReader); Futurism.

## Draft interview

## INTERVIEW: THE MIKE, ON CENSORSHIP

*PARODY. This interview never happened. Mike Pondsmith did not say this. Some of the ideas are his, from interviews on the record; the words are ours.*

You can't summon a living man at the Afterlife. So I didn't order a drink. I asked the question out loud at the bar: who decides what Night City gets to say? Then a man sat down who looked like he'd been asked that question before and had a better answer than I did.

He wore the expression of a game master watching players open the wrong door on purpose.

**CT:** You made this city.

**MP:** I made a game. You're the ones who keep living in it.

**CT:** Let's talk about censorship. Who shuts people up in Night City?

**MP:** Who owns the screens?

**CT:** The corps.

**MP:** Then you've answered it. Nobody in Night City needs a censor. You don't have to ban a story if you own every place it could be told. You just don't run it. Or you run it next to an ad so loud nobody hears it.

**CT:** So the corps are the censors.

**MP:** The corps are the landlords. Censorship is what landlords call maintenance.

**CT:** And the Media? In your rules, the Media's whole job is getting the truth out.

**MP:** The Media's job is being believed. Truth's the easy part; everybody's got some. Getting people to believe you, when the people with the screens are telling them not to, that's the fight.

**CT:** People call Cyberpunk a dream. Chrome, neon, guns.

**MP:** It's a warning. It was always a warning. You're not supposed to want to live there. You're supposed to look at it and go, we'd better not.

**CT:** Some people say a game shouldn't be political.

**MP:** Everything's political. Just not the way people think, left and right. It's about power and who has it and what they do to the people who don't.

**CT:** Then this zine is doing your work. Calling out the corps. Following the money. Isn't it?

**MP:** *(smiling)* Here's the thing. You want somebody to see your point of view? Don't preach. Nobody ever changed their mind because a zine yelled at them. Show them. Put them in the room. Let them look at the guy with the money and figure it out themselves.

**CT:** That's what the photos are for.

**MP:** Then trust the photos. Write less.

**CT:** One more. Style over substance. Attitude is everything. Always take it to the edge. Break the rules. Those are yours.

**MP:** Those are for players.

**CT:** I'm a player.

**MP:** Then play. *(He stands.)* And kid. When somebody tells you a story can't be told, that's usually the story.

He didn't leave by the door either. I think he was never in the room. That's the thing about the people who build the city. They're always just outside the frame.
