# Interview prep: Richard and Miriam Night

**Status by 2077:** Richard is dead, murdered in 1998. Miriam's fate wasn't found.
**Cyberpunk 2020 source:** not confirmed in the sources found. Check the 2020 *Night City* sourcebook.
**Format:** the two of them talk mostly to each other. The interviewer barely gets in.

## What canon says
- **Richard Night** founded the city on Del Coronado Bay. Sources put the founding in the early 1990s; one says 1994.
- It was Coronado City. After his murder, the new City Council renamed it Night City in his honor.
- He was murdered on September 20, 1998, in his penthouse at Parkview Tower (one source). The killer was never caught.
- **Miriam Night,** his widow, restructured his company into the Night's Foundation in 1999. One source says his company was first Night International or Night Industries. It later became Night Corp.
- In 2077 the city carries his name, and Night Corp is a corporation.
- His vision of a planned, better city comes up often in lore summaries but wasn't sourced here. The draft uses it as his own story about himself, which Miriam picks apart.

Sources: TechRadar, Cyberpunk 2077 lore guide; Space4Games, Cyberpunk 2077 timeline; other search results (dates vary).

## Follow the money
The man who planned the city is killed. His company passes to his widow and becomes a corporation. The city takes his name. The killer is never found. The interview never answers who did it. It lets them circle it.

## Draft interview

## INTERVIEW WITH THE DEAD: RICHARD AND MIRIAM NIGHT

The Afterlife has no drink for the man the city is named after. It's a merc bar; he was never a merc. I ordered a glass of champagne and a glass of water and put them side by side, and I asked Claire not to ask.

They came in together, from two different years. Richard Night from 1998, in a good suit cut for a man who expected to be photographed beside a model of something. Miriam Night from some year after, in black that had been black a long time. She sat first. He took the stool beside her like he'd never had to ask for a seat in his life.

**CT:** Mr. Night. Mrs. Night.

**RN:** *(looking around)* What is this place?

**MN:** A bar for mercenaries, Richard. In your city.

**RN:** My city didn't have bars for mercenaries. My city had parks. I had the plans drawn. Walkable districts. Clean power. The whole bay, laid out like a promise.

**MN:** You had the plans drawn. Then you had the investors.

**RN:** You need investors to build a city.

**MN:** You need investors to own one.

**CT:** I'd like to ask about the money.

**RN:** *(to Miriam)* There. You see? Everyone wants to talk about the money.

**MN:** Because there was so much of it, darling.

**RN:** I built Coronado City because the old cities were dying. Crime, bankruptcy, the government asleep at the wheel. I said, let's build one that works. Everyone said I was a dreamer.

**MN:** Everyone said you were a developer. The dreaming was for the brochure.

**RN:** You loved the brochure.

**MN:** I wrote the brochure.

**CT:** Mr. Night, who killed you?

*(They both look at me. Then at each other.)*

**RN:** The police never found out.

**MN:** The police never asked the right people.

**RN:** Which people?

**MN:** The people who were at the meetings you didn't invite me to.

**RN:** Those were business meetings.

**MN:** That's what I said.

**CT:** After he died, the company became a foundation. Then a corporation.

**MN:** Somebody had to hold it together. You know what happens to a dead man's company? The vultures don't wait for the funeral. I put it in a foundation so they'd have to get through me.

**RN:** And then?

**MN:** And then the foundation needed money. And money wants a corporation. And here we are.

**RN:** You put my name on a corp.

**MN:** They put your name on a city. I just kept up.

**RN:** I never wanted this. Look at it. *(He turns on the stool to face the window and the lights.)* Towers with logos on the top. Gangs on the bottom. Nobody in between.

**MN:** You wanted towers with your logo on the top.

**RN:** That's different.

**MN:** It's the same logo, Richard.

**CT:** This zine says the city's run by the people who profit from it. You two are the first names on the ledger.

**RN:** *(to Miriam)* Is it saying we're the villains?

**MN:** It's saying we're the founders. In this city, it's the same paragraph.

**RN:** *(to me, finally)* You think you can do better? Build a city. Go on. See who comes to the ribbon cutting, and see who sends flowers to the funeral.

**MN:** *(standing)* He's not building anything, dear. He's writing it down. That's worse for us.

She drank the water. He didn't touch the champagne. When they left, they left in different directions.
