# Off-Island Supply: What Has to Come From Abroad

Companion to `infrastructureHubPortAuPrince.md` and `infrastructureHubEconomics.md`. This file covers four questions:
- Which resources for the buildout and for ongoing operation have to come from off the island?
- What can Haiti make for itself?
- How do we keep the import routes from becoming chokepoints?
- How are the imports paid for?

**Bottom line:**
- **Nearly every high-tech component comes from abroad, and always will:** reactor modules and fuel, solar and battery cells, chips and servers, water-treatment membranes, medical equipment and most medicines, and rail vehicles. Haiti contributes labor, sun, sea, limestone, organic waste, and, once the hub is built, cheap power.
- **The heaviest materials, cement and steel, can be made locally once the power exists.** That is the biggest win from local production. Haiti imported about $95M of cement in 2022, mostly from the Dominican Republic and Turkey.
- **Import routes are Utility Valves too.**
  - The Port-au-Prince port suspended operations in 2024 after gangs attacked and looted it.
  - Deportation flights land in Cap-Haïtien because the capital's airport is too dangerous.
  - Gangs reportedly control the land routes into the city.
  - The 2022 Varreux fuel-terminal blockade showed what happens when one entry point is captured.

  So the hub needs its own secure port, more than one route for everything critical, and stockpiles.
- **The currency lines up.** Imports are paid in dollars, and export revenue is earned in dollars. The hub also cuts Haiti's fuel import bill (about $525M of refined petroleum in 2022, mostly from the US) as EVs, electric cooking, and reactor power displace diesel and heavy fuel oil.

## 1. Buildout: what must be imported

| System | Imported | Made or done in Haiti |
|---|---|---|
| **Reactor complex** | Power modules and their fuel; the research reactor with its fuel and isotope targets; instrumentation and control systems; dry casks for spent fuel; hot cells for handling radioactive material; the cyclotron; linear accelerators for radiotherapy | Civil works (caverns, concrete) and installation labor under vendor supervision |
| **Grid** | Large power transformers (2–5 year lead time, so order first), switchgear, high-voltage cable, meters, protection relays | Poles, trenching, distribution lines, installation |
| **Solar and storage** | Solar panels, inverters, battery cells and battery systems | Racking fabricated from imported aluminum or steel, canopy structures, installation |
| **Data center and library** | Servers, AI chips (GPUs), disk and tape storage, network equipment, cooling plant, backup power, and the subsea cable plus the ship that lays it | Building shell, cable landing works, installing the racks |
| **Water** | Desalination membranes, high-pressure pumps, energy-recovery devices, distillation units, valves | Tanks, intake works, pipe-laying; disinfectant made on site from salt water |
| **Sanitation and waste** | Mixers and gas-handling equipment for the digesters, composting and sorting machinery, the first batch of sealed toilet containers | Concrete digester tanks, treatment ponds, and later containers molded from recycled plastic |
| **Rail and cable cars** | Light rail vehicles, rails, signaling, traction power, cable-car systems, steel tendons for prestressed concrete | Precast concrete girders, piers, stations |
| **Buildings** | Seismic base isolators, elevators, air conditioning and ventilation, glazing | Concrete structures, block, finishing, furniture |
| **Vehicles** | Slate trucks, Zero motorcycles, e-bike motors and battery cells | Assembling e-bikes and battery packs |
| **Hospital and college** | Imaging, lab analyzers, radiotherapy machines, the cyclotron, IT, lab equipment | Fit-out, furniture, textbook printing |
| **Expertise (early years)** | Reactor vendor engineers, IAEA advisers, international faculty, accreditation reviews | Need for outside help shrinks as graduates take over |

**Order the long-lead items first:**
- Reactor modules (years).
- Large power transformers (2–5 years as of 2026).
- The subsea cable (cable-laying ships are scarce and booked well ahead).
- Rail vehicles and cable-car systems.
- Radiotherapy machines and the cyclotron.

## 2. Ongoing operation: what keeps coming

| Item | How often | Can Haiti make it? |
|---|---|---|
| Fuel for the power reactors | Each module about every 2 years, staggered | **Never.** The charter rules out enrichment and fuel fabrication. |
| Research reactor fuel and isotope targets | Continuous cycles | **Never** |
| Spent fuel | Shipped out under take-back | — |
| Oxygen-18-enriched water for the cyclotron (raw material for PET isotopes) | Ongoing | No |
| Medicines, vaccines, medical supplies | Continuous | Mostly no. Medical oxygen yes, from on-site oxygen plants. |
| Electronics replacement: GPUs and servers (every 5–6 years), disks (about 5), inverters (10–15), batteries (about 15), solar panels (30+), network equipment | On replacement cycles | Assembly and repair only |
| Desalination membranes and pump parts | Membranes about every 5–7 years | No |
| Water-treatment chemicals | Continuous | Chlorine yes; scale inhibitors no |
| Rail and cable-car parts: wheels, brakes, signaling electronics, haul ropes | On replacement cycles | Partly, through local machining |
| Hub vehicle parts: tires, brake parts, motor controllers, battery packs | Continuous | Partly: e-bike and battery-pack assembly |
| Lubricants, refrigerants, filters, protective equipment | Continuous | Mostly no |
| Cement and steel for maintenance | Continuous | **Yes,** once local plants run (Section 3) |
| Software and firmware updates, vendor support | Continuous | Open source where possible; vendor contracts for the reactors |
| People coming in: IAEA inspectors (by design), vendor crews for reactor outages, accreditation reviewers | Periodic | Decreasing as Haitians qualify |

## 3. What Haiti can make

Cheap power plus local raw materials make it practical to produce the heavy, bulky, and simple items on the island:

1. **Cement.**
   - Local limestone feeds the kiln, fired partly by fuel made from the Processing Yard's waste, as the waste plan already proposes.
   - This replaces an import worth about $95M a year (2022).
   - **Quarries stay out of the Massif de la Selle,** which supplies the city's water.
2. **Rebar.** An electric-arc furnace mini-mill melts scrap steel from old vehicles and demolition. It uses a lot of electricity, which is exactly what the reactor supplies.
3. **Precast concrete:** girders, station parts, tanks, and concrete pipe.
4. **Recycled plastic** turned into sanitation containers, non-drinking-water pipe, and crates. The plastic-for-credits program supplies the feedstock.
5. **Chlorine disinfectant,** made by running electricity through salt water.
6. **Medical oxygen,** from on-site oxygen plants.
7. **Compost, biogas, and biomethane.**
8. **Assembly work:**
   - E-bikes.
   - Battery packs from imported cells.
   - Solar racking.
   - Electrical panels.

   Haiti's existing garment-assembly workforce is a starting base.
9. **Medical isotopes.** Once the research reactor runs, Mo-99/Tc-99m and Lu-177 are made locally. Mo-99 has a 66-hour half-life, so it can also be flown to nearby Caribbean hospitals.
10. **Knowledge:** curricula, software, and data, through the library.
11. **Food, in part.** About 80–85% of Haiti's rice is imported. The hub won't change that alone, but compost, irrigation water, and cold storage help local farming (see `infrastructureHubFood.md`).

**What will always be imported:** chips, solar and battery cells, reactor fuel, membranes, most medicines, and precision instruments. The plan is to accept that and manage it.

## 4. Keeping the routes open

**Current routes and their problems:**

| Route | Status |
|---|---|
| Port-au-Prince port | Suspended operations in 2024 after gangs attacked and looted it |
| Port-au-Prince airport | Deportation flights land in Cap-Haïtien instead because the capital's airport is too dangerous |
| Cap-Haïtien port and airport | Working, in the north |
| Land border with the Dominican Republic | Haiti has banned some Dominican goods by road, and gangs reportedly hold the land routes into the capital |
| **North Campus jetty** | **New, built by the hub** |

**Recommendations:**
1. **Make the North Campus jetty the hub's own secure port.** It needs a heavy-lift berth for reactor modules and transformers, container handling, and nuclear fuel delivery. It sits outside the city and inside the protected perimeter that Response Teams may guard as critical infrastructure.
2. **At least two routes for everything critical:** the North Campus jetty and Cap-Haïtien, with the Dominican Republic border as a third. Regional shipping and outbound cargo are covered in `infrastructureHubMaritime.md`.
3. **Twelve months of stockpiles on the island.**
   - What's stored: critical spares and supplies, including membranes, inverter boards, medical supplies, water chemicals, and vehicle parts.
   - Where: warehouses at the North Campus that withstand hurricanes and floods, plus smaller stocks at stations.
   - Reactor fuel: each reload arrives well before its outage.
4. **More than one supplier for every critical item.** Solar and battery cells come overwhelmingly from China, so line up at least two sources. Reactor fuel comes only from suppliers that aren't under sanctions.
5. **Clean customs.**
   - Designate the hub a free zone, with duty exemptions for donated and operating imports.
   - Publish every shipment's manifest on the public ledger, so goods can't quietly go missing at customs.

## 5. Paying for imports

- **Running the hub needs foreign currency** for fuel, spare parts, equipment replacement, and medicines.
- **Export revenue is earned in dollars** (Export Teams, partner students, the data center), so the hub's currency needs match its income. The replacement fund is held in hard currency by the endowment trust abroad.
- **The hub reduces Haiti's imports:**
  - **Fuel:** every EV, electric stove, and megawatt-hour of reactor power displaces imported diesel and heavy fuel oil, part of a $525M refined-petroleum import bill (2022).
  - **Charcoal:** electric cooking cuts its use.
  - **Cement:** local production replaces about $95M a year.

## 6. Changes to the build order

- **Phase 0:**
  - Place the long-lead orders: transformers, subsea cable, rail vehicles, and reactor production slots.
  - Set up the free zone and transparent customs.
  - Design the North Campus jetty.
- **Phase 1:**
  - Import solar, batteries, and sanitation equipment through existing routes.
  - Start building the stockpile.
- **Phase 2:**
  - Open the North Campus jetty before any heavy equipment arrives.
  - Start recycled plastics, chlorine production, and oxygen plants.
- **Phase 4:** begin local isotope production at the research reactor.
- **Phase 5:** build the cement plant and the steel mini-mill once reactor power makes them cheap.
