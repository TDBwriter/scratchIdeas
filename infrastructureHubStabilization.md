# Stabilization Model: Community Teams & Response Teams

Companion to `infrastructureHubPortAuPrince.md` and `infrastructureHubSolarMobility.md`. This file captures the security concept for the buildout:

- **Tactical control is claimed as each stabilization buildout goes in:** infrastructure, community clinics, and resource centers.
- **Teams are modeled on Green Beret Operational Detachment Alphas (ODAs)** and built around community educators, leaders, and medical personnel.
- **Locals are trained to serve their communities, with no role in violence.**
- **Armed teams respond only to active threats,** using precise force against hostile actors.
- **Lethal force is authorized only where the escalation is enemy-led.**
- **There is no general occupying military presence and no policing.**

**Bottom line:** The concept holds up, but only as **two separate teams behind a firewall**, not one blended team.
- **Community Teams** are unarmed, local, and organized like an ODA. They build and run the services.
- **Response Teams** are armed and ODA-sized. They answer active threats under **Haitian state authority and the UN-mandated force, never as the hub's own force.**

Three conditions make it work:
1. "Surgical" is proven with public data, not assumed.
2. Capture and trial must be realistic options; otherwise precise force slides into extrajudicial killing.
3. The resource centers provide an exit for the 30–50% of armed-group members who are children.

---

## 1. What already exists in Port-au-Prince

- **The response role is already authorized.** UN Security Council Resolution 2793 (September 2025) created the Gang Suppression Force under Chapter VII. Its mandate covers "intelligence-led targeted, counter-gang operations to neutralise, isolate, and deter gangs," securing critical infrastructure and transit routes, and arresting suspects. **The hub doesn't need to invent an armed force.** It needs to align its buildouts with this mandate and with Haitian forces.
- **"Surgical" has already been tested here.** Human Rights Watch counted at least 1,243 people killed in 141 drone operations (March 2025 to January 2026). The strikes were carried out by a task force set up by the Prime Minister and supported by the US contractor Vectus Global. At least 17 children and 43 other adults not believed to be gang members were among the dead. Precision weapons in dense neighborhoods still killed bystanders.
- **Medical neutrality has already broken down, on both sides.**
  - December 2023: armed men stopped an ambulance and killed a patient, and Médecins Sans Frontières (MSF) closed its Turgeau emergency centre.
  - November 2024: police and vigilantes attacked an MSF ambulance and executed at least two patients. MSF then suspended all work in Port-au-Prince for the first time in about 30 years.
- **Many of the armed actors are children.** The UN estimates that 30–50% of armed-group members are children, and recruitment tripled in 2025.
- **When locals take up violence, it becomes vigilantism.** The 2023 *Bwa Kale* movement, in which crowds killed suspected gang members, shows why locals should have no role in violence.
- **Haiti's exit path for armed-group members is weak.** The national disarmament and reintegration commission (CNDDR) was reorganized in 2025, but UN human rights reports still urge the government to re-establish an effective national body.

## 2. Two teams, one firewall

| | **Community Team** | **Response Team** |
|---|---|---|
| **Purpose** | Build and run clinics, resource centers, kiosks, sanitation, solar, and education | Respond to active threats to life |
| **People** | Local hires, trained by the college | A Haitian-led specialized unit, with foreign special-forces personnel as advisers only |
| **Armed** | Never | Yes |
| **Authority** | The hub's civic charter and neighborhood councils | The Haitian state and a UN Security Council mandate, or a UN peacekeeping mission with Haitian-led units (see `infrastructureHubSovereignty.md`, Section 9) |
| **Funding** | The hub (grant, endowment, at-cost revenue) | Public and UN channels. **No hub money, vehicles, or data.** |
| **Base** | Stations and resource centers | Separate compounds, never civic sites |
| **Appearance** | Clearly civilian; no uniforms or camouflage | Uniformed and visibly distinct |
| **Data** | Service data only | Its own sources, never Community Team data |

**Why the teams must be separate, not one integrated team:**
- **Armed medics lose their protected status.** Under the Geneva Conventions, medical personnel are protected only if they do medical work exclusively. A Green Beret medic is a combatant. A clinic run by an armed team is a military target.
- **Community staff seen as part of an armed force face retaliation.** Separation protects the locals who, by design, have no role in violence.
- **Humanitarian practice treats armed escorts as a last resort** (the inter-agency guidelines on armed escorts). An escort marks the people escorted as part of the force.

**Firewall rules:**
1. **No shared sites.** Response Teams never stage in, sleep in, or store weapons at clinics, schools, resource centers, or a station's public spaces. They may guard the perimeters of critical infrastructure, such as the North Campus and substations, but never civic spaces.
2. **No intelligence from civic work.** Community Teams never report on individuals. Response Teams can't access clinic records, school rolls, ledger data, or hub fleet logs without a court order. The hub's ledger knows what everyone uses and where, which would make it a ready-made targeting database, so the firewall must be technical (collect less data, encrypt it, and lock access behind keys) as well as legal. **Precedent:** the CIA's fake vaccination campaign in Pakistan in 2011 led to attacks on polio workers, and the US pledged in 2014 to stop using vaccination programs for intelligence.
3. **Medical neutrality.** Clinics treat everyone by medical need alone, including wounded hostile actors and Response Team members. No armed person enters a clinic unless stopping an active attack.
4. **The fleet stays civilian.** No hub vehicle (Slate, Zero, or e-bike) is ever used for operations.
5. **No one points anyone out.** Community Team members are never asked to identify anyone. Their protection comes from neutrality and community ownership.

## 3. Community Team: an ODA without weapons

The 12-person ODA maps onto a civic team. The two Weapons Sergeant slots become educators; that is the one deliberate change.

| ODA role | Community Team role | Who fills it |
|---|---|---|
| 18A Detachment Commander | **Team Lead** | A locally chosen community leader |
| 180A Assistant Detachment Commander | **Deputy and continuity lead** | Local; steps up if the lead leaves, so the team survives political transitions |
| 18Z Operations Sergeant | **Operations coordinator** | Runs the station and resource center day to day |
| 18F Intelligence Sergeant | **Community needs analyst** | Tracks needs and service gaps, **never information on individuals** |
| 18B Weapons Sergeant ×2 | **Educators ×2** | Schools, literacy, apprenticeship intake, health education |
| 18C Engineer Sergeant ×2 | **Infrastructure technicians ×2** | Solar, batteries, water kiosk, sanitation containers |
| 18D Medical Sergeant ×2 | **Clinicians ×2** | A nurse and a community health worker |
| 18E Communications Sergeant ×2 | **Communications and data ×2** | Radio, telehealth link, early warning, public information in Kreyòl |

**ODA traits the team keeps:**
- **Small:** 12 people.
- **Cross-trained:** each pair can cover the other's work.
- **Self-sufficient for days:** stations hold their own reserves.
- **Embedded for years, not months.**
- **Language and culture first.**
- **"By, with, and through" the community:** each team trains its successors and works itself out of a job.

**Pipeline:**
- The college trains the first teams, and each team then trains the next wave in its own community.
- Diaspora professionals and international clinicians serve as advisers, never as leads.

## 4. Response Team: response-only and accountable

Your limits, written as rules:
- **Respond only to active threats to life.** No patrols that amount to policing, no occupation, no neighborhood raids, and no kill lists.
- **Lethal force only where the escalation is enemy-led,** and only when strictly unavoidable to protect life. The full rule is below. It meets the UN Basic Principles on the Use of Force and Firearms. It is also stricter than the law of armed conflict, which allows targeting armed-group members by status alone if a conflict is recognized. Legal advisers confirm which body of law applies; the rule holds either way.
- **Capture over killing whenever feasible.** Detainees go to the Haitian National Police and the courts within legal time limits, with no secret detention. **This requires a working justice system.** The 2024 mass breakout from the National Penitentiary shows the gap. Without courts and secure detention, capture becomes impossible and pressure to kill rises. Justice capacity is part of the buildout.
- **No explosive weapons with wide-area effects in populated areas.** The drone record above shows why. More than 80 states endorsed the 2022 political declaration on explosive weapons in populated areas.
- **Child protocols.** Assume any combatant may be a child. Separate children, protect them, and hand them to Haiti's social welfare institute (IBESR), not to detention.
- **Haitian-led.** Teams are modeled on the ODA and staffed by Haitian specialized units, with foreign special forces as advisers only. Haiti's history makes foreign command unacceptable: the US occupation of 1915–1934, and the cholera epidemic linked to the 2004–2017 UN mission.
- **Small footprint.** Teams respond from staging compounds; they don't occupy every street.

### Lethal force: only when the escalation is enemy-led

The Response Team never escalates first. Lethal force is authorized only after a hostile actor has escalated to lethal violence. Authorization is based on **what a person is doing at that moment, not who they are.**

**What counts as enemy-led escalation:**
- **A hostile act.** Someone is using deadly force against anyone: residents, Community Team staff, Response Team members, or patients.
- **An imminent threat.** Someone is clearly and immediately about to use deadly force.
- **A continuing hostile act.** Hostage-taking, or an armed siege of a clinic, station, or school, remains an escalation until it ends.
- **An attack on infrastructure, only if it puts lives at immediate risk.** Cutting power to a hospital's life support counts. Damage to property alone never authorizes lethal force.

**What never counts:**
- **Status:** membership in an armed group, reputation, or appearing on a list. This rules out planned strikes on people who aren't escalating at that moment, such as the drone strikes on gatherings.
- **Carrying a weapon** without threatening anyone.
- **Fleeing, refusing orders, or committing property crime.**
- **Escalation the team provoked.** No entering an area to draw fire, and no baiting.

**How the rule is applied:**
- **Before lethal force, where time allows:** warnings, de-escalation, non-lethal options, and withdrawal if withdrawing protects lives.
- **Force goes only against the person escalating,** never the whole group or the people nearby.
- **Authorization ends when the escalation ends.** Anyone who surrenders, is wounded and out of the fight, or stops threatening others is protected and gets arrest and medical care, not lethal force. Pursuit is for arrest only.
- **The burden of proof is on the team.** Every lethal-force report must show what the other side did first, backed by body-camera footage and independent review. If it can't show that, the force is treated as unlawful and the halt rule applies.

**Partners:** The hub coordinates only with forces that accept this rule at or near hub sites. That is a stricter standard than the Gang Suppression Force mandate, which allows targeted operations.

**Making "surgical" provable:**
- Log every use of force; use body cameras; require independent review.
- Publish civilian harm tracking (counts, locations, investigations) on the public ledger.
- Provide **independent oversight:**
  - The Office for the Protection of Citizens (OPC), Haiti's ombudsman.
  - UN human rights reporting (BINUH).
  - An anonymous complaints channel at every station, which the Response Team can't trace.
- **A halt rule.** Operations in an area pause pending review if any of these happens:
  - Civilian harm crosses a set threshold.
  - An investigation finds unlawful force.
  - A lethal-force report can't show enemy-led escalation.
- **The hub's response to violations.** If a force breaks the firewall or the rules of engagement, the hub's response is publicity and withdrawal of partnership. **It never cuts utilities;** the lifeline floors apply to everyone.

## 5. Claiming control and sequencing the buildout

- **Ink spots.** Buildouts start where control already exists. Each station and resource center becomes a secure node, and the secure area spreads outward along the rail spine.
- **Clear, hold, build:**
  - State and UN forces clear and hold under their mandate.
  - Community Teams build.
  - Services and community ownership sustain the hold, not a standing armed presence.
- **Handing off to normal life.** An area moves from Response Team coverage to normal civilian life when measured indicators hold for a set period: incidents, school attendance, clinic use, market activity. Then the Response Team steps back, and Haitian National Police community policing takes over. Policing is the police's job, not the Response Team's.
- **Community Teams go only where they can work unescorted.** Phase 1 civic projects follow that rule.

## 6. Exit paths: the resource centers

- **Why exits matter:** 30–50% of armed-group members are children, and recruitment tripled in 2025. Without a way out, every "hostile actor" may be a child casualty, and the next recruit replaces them.
- **Resource centers host the intake for disarmament and violence reduction,** in partnership with the national DDR body, which the UN says must be rebuilt:
  - **The state defines amnesty and eligibility,** not the hub.
  - **Weapons are handed in at a separate police-run site.** Community Teams never handle weapons.
  - **Jobs are the hub's strongest offer:** solar, battery, sanitation, and rail apprenticeships, with priority for young people at risk of recruitment, plus school meals.
  - **The firewall still applies.** Intake records never go to Response Teams, and people enrolled in the program are protected from targeting under rules set by the state.

## 7. Link to governance

- **The Multi-Key Council holds no key over armed force, and the armed force holds no key over utilities.** Putting the Utility Valve and weapons in the same hands is exactly the "Moral Coup" the blueprint's Section III warns about.
- **Donors fund civic work only.** No hub money, vehicles, or data go to armed units. That keeps the hub from becoming a private military, a live issue given the state-licensed private contractor behind the drone campaign.
- **For the story:** the firewall is where the drama is. Every crisis will tempt someone to cross it: a medic asked to name a patient, a ledger record subpoenaed, a council member who wants the force to answer to the keys.
